The evolution of artificial intelligence in the workplace is transitioning from a conversational model—where users ask questions and receive answers—to an agentic model, where AI systems are delegated multi-step tasks to perform actions on a user's behalf. While traditional large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT have primarily functioned as sophisticated consultants, agentic AI tools like OpenAI’s Codex are increasingly used to execute code, analyze data, and manage complex workflows.

This shift represents a fundamental change in how humans interact with technology. Instead of merely seeking information, users are now managing autonomous systems that produce artifacts and perform technical operations. By analyzing large-scale usage data across diverse populations—including internal OpenAI employees, external organizations, and individual users—researchers have begun to document the characteristics of this agentic transition.

## Defining the Agentic Shift

The primary distinction between conversational AI and agentic AI lies in the nature of the interaction. In a conversational setting, the AI acts as a respondent. In an agentic setting, the AI acts as a producer or an operator. Codex, the platform studied in this research, allows users to delegate tasks that involve autonomous file inspection, command execution, and the creation of complex technical artifacts.

The research identifies three main user populations to understand this diffusion:
1.  **Individual Users:** Early adopters using personal accounts.
2.  **Organizational Users:** Employees using enterprise-level accounts where corporate security and specific business workflows are factors.
3.  **OpenAI Internal Users:** A "frontier" population with high technical literacy and low barriers to tool adoption, providing a glimpse into the potential future of broader market use.

In the first half of 2026, Codex usage expanded significantly. Within OpenAI, Codex became the dominant tool for work-related AI, accounting for $99.8\%$ of all output tokens generated by employees. In the broader organizational market, Codex captured $63.3\%$ of the token share, while for individual users, it represented $16.5\%$. This disparity suggests that the value of agentic AI is currently most realized in technical and organizational contexts where complex delegation is possible.

## Mapping the Diffusion of Agentic Tools

While Codex was originally conceived as a tool for software developers, its adoption pattern shows it is rapidly moving into non-technical domains. Within OpenAI, adoption became nearly universal across departments—including legal, recruiting, and sales—by mid-2026.

The data indicates that agentic AI is not confined to entry-level or junior roles. Adoption is observed across all seniority levels, from individual contributors to executives. This suggests that the tasks being delegated are not merely "grunt work" but include high-level planning, research, and review processes.

## Measuring Task Complexity and Human Delegation

One of the most informative findings of this research is the increase in the complexity of tasks assigned to AI agents. Researchers utilized a classifier to estimate the "human-equivalent time" required to complete a given task without AI assistance. This provides a metric for the degree of work being offloaded to the system.

In late 2025, approximately $35.4\%$ of active individual users submitted at least one prompt estimated to take a human expert at least one hour to complete. By May 2026, this figure rose to $70.2\%$. Even more striking is the growth in highly complex tasks: the share of users delegating work that would take a human more than eight hours to complete increased from $2.1\%$ to $25.6\%$.

![figure-3-task-complexity-thresholds-over-time](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.26959v1/codex_request_complexity_user_threshold_shares_consumer_monthly.png)
*{fig:figure-3-task-complexity-thresholds-over-time}: The percentage of users delegating tasks that exceed specific human-time benchmarks. The rapid increase in the 8-hour+ category signifies a shift toward delegating substantial projects rather than simple queries.*

This increase in complexity follows a specific pattern within user sessions. Users tend to delegate the most complex, broad portions of a project at the beginning of a thread, using subsequent interactions to refine or validate the results. This indicates that agentic AI is being used as a primary engine for production rather than a secondary source of advice.

## The Expansion of Work Areas

The transition to agentic AI is also characterized by a broadening of the types of work being performed. While software-related tasks—such as code implementation and engineering operations—remain the largest share of Codex activity, other knowledge-work domains are growing.

Commonly delegated tasks now include:
*   **Knowledge Artifacts:** Drafting documents, presentations, and reports.
*   **Research and Planning:** Analyzing internal documentation and creating project outlines.
*   **Data Analysis:** Running complex queries and interpreting large datasets.
*   **Collaboration:** Drafting communications and managing team workflows.

In the OpenAI "frontier" case, tasks related to research and application management represent a significant portion of the total workload. This suggests that as users become more comfortable with agentic tools, they find ways to apply them to virtually every aspect of knowledge work.

## New Workflows: Concurrency and Systematization

The shift to agentic AI is fundamentally altering how workers organize their time. Unlike conversational AI, which usually involves a one-to-one exchange, agentic AI allows for parallel execution.

In the study, "turn concurrency" measures how many different AI tasks a user has running simultaneously. Within the high-adoption OpenAI population, $28.6\%$ of users managed five or more concurrent agents weekly. In contrast, most external users still operate in a linear fashion, with $67.4\%$ of organizational users running only one task at a time. This gap highlights a significant potential for productivity growth as users learn to oversee multiple parallel workstreams.

![figure-5-peak-concurrent-turns-by-account-category](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.26959v1/turn_concurrency_by_account_category_new.png)
*{fig:figure-5-peak-concurrent-turns-by-account-category}: This chart shows the distribution of concurrent agent usage. Advanced users at OpenAI frequently manage 10 or more parallel tasks, a behavior not yet common in the broader market.*

Furthermore, users are increasingly "systematizing" their work through the use of skills and plugins. These are reusable sets of instructions or tool integrations that provide the AI with persistent context. By June 2026, $96.2\%$ of OpenAI Codex users were using these systematized tools, compared to about $30\%$ of organizational users. This suggests that the future of agentic AI involves building personal or departmental "libraries" of automated capabilities that can be triggered for repeated tasks.

## Implications for Productivity and Workforce Structure

The data from the internal OpenAI population suggests a potential explosion in individual output. For example, the median output tokens—a proxy for work produced—increased 13-fold for legal roles and 50-fold for researchers over a seven-month period. While output tokens are not a perfect measure of productivity, they indicate a massive increase in the volume of work being processed and generated.

This shift necessitates a change in how we measure work. If an employee can oversee multiple agents that accumulate 71 hours of "runtime" in a single calendar day (as seen in the 99th percentile of OpenAI users), the traditional 8-hour workday metric becomes obsolete. Instead, productivity may be measured by the complexity of the delegated tasks and the effectiveness of the human's supervision and verification.

## Organizational Complements and Future Outlook

The research emphasizes that the transition to agentic AI is not just about the technology itself, but about the "organizational complements" that support it. The high adoption at OpenAI is likely driven by a combination of high technical skill, cultural buy-in, and the development of internal processes for reviewing and integrating AI-generated work.

For broader organizations, the path to realizing these gains likely involves:
1.  **Skill Development:** Training workers to move from "doing" to "delegating" and "verifying."
2.  **Workflow Redesign:** Adapting business processes to accommodate parallel workstreams and high-volume output.
3.  **Tool Integration:** Moving beyond generic chat interfaces to specialized agentic platforms that can access internal data and systems.

As agentic AI continues to diffuse, the role of the human worker will likely evolve into that of a "manager of agents." This requires a shift in focus toward judgment, quality control, and strategic coordination. The evidence from Codex suggests that we are in the early stages of a significant reorganization of knowledge work, where the primary value of AI is not in what it says, but in what it does.
