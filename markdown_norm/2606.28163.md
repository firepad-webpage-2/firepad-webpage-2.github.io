## The Evolution of Video Compression and the INR Paradigm

Video compression remains a cornerstone of digital infrastructure, enabling the transmission of high-definition content across diverse networks. Traditionally, this field has been dominated by block-based hybrid codecs like H.264/AVC, HEVC, and the more recent VVC. These standards rely on hand-crafted algorithms for motion estimation, transform coding, and entropy modeling. In recent years, deep learning has introduced a new paradigm: Neural Video Compression.

Neural codecs are generally split into two categories. Autoencoder-based methods transform video frames into a compact latent space, while Implicit Neural Representation (INR) methods represent the video as the parameters of a neural network. In the INR framework, the video is "overfitted" into a model such that the network maps coordinates (temporal and spatial) to pixel values. Compression is then achieved by quantizing and entropy-coding the model's weights and feature grids.

*{fig:nvrc-framework-overview}: The NVRC++ framework integrates hierarchical feature grids, an in-parameter coding structure for temporal dependencies, and the HiNeRV++ architecture to achieve high-performance video compression.*

While INR-based codecs like NeRV, HiNeRV, and NVRC have shown impressive decoding speeds, they have historically struggled with scalability. As users demand higher video quality, the computational complexity and model size of these codecs often increase significantly. This makes it difficult to deploy a single architecture across devices with varying hardware capabilities. NVRC++ addresses these limitations by introducing a unified architecture that maintains consistent decoding complexity while scaling across a wide range of bitrates and quality levels.

## Architecture: HiNeRV++ and Multi-Resolution Feature Grids

The core of the NVRC++ framework is the HiNeRV++ architecture, an improved version of the earlier HiNeRV model. To represent a video, the system uses a set of learnable feature embeddings stored in multi-resolution grids. For any given coordinate in the video, features are extracted from these grids using trilinear interpolation and then processed through a neural network to reconstruct the frame.

The framework defines its parameters as $\theta = \{\theta_{grid}, \theta_{layer}\}$, where $\theta_{grid}$ represents the spatial-temporal feature grids and $\theta_{layer}$ represents the weights of the neural network layers. The primary innovation in HiNeRV++ is the strategic use of high-resolution grids to offload the representational burden from the network layers. By using larger grids, the network itself can remain lightweight, which significantly accelerates decoding.

HiNeRV++ introduces several structural changes to maximize efficiency:
1.  **Multi-Reference Inputs:** Unlike previous models that rely only on interpolated features, HiNeRV++ takes multiple neighboring raw feature slices from the grid as input. This provides the model with higher-fidelity information about the temporal context.
2.  **Extended Feature Integration:** Feature encodings are injected into the first three stages of the network blocks, rather than just the initial stage. This allows the model to refine details progressively.
3.  **Simplified Output Layer:** The final high-resolution blocks and head layers found in earlier models are replaced with a single Pixel Shuffle layer. This reduction in depth at the highest resolution stage minimizes the computational cost per pixel, which is essential for maintaining high frame rates.

## The In-Parameter Coding Structure and Optimization

A major challenge in INR video compression is managing the massive amount of data in high-resolution feature grids. In NVRC++, these grids are partitioned into temporal slices, denoted as $z_t$. To exploit temporal redundancy—the fact that consecutive frames in a video are often very similar—the framework introduces an "in-parameter coding structure."

This structure is inspired by the hierarchical B-frame arrangement used in traditional codecs. Instead of coding each slice independently, the model establishes dependencies between them. A quantized slice $\hat{z}_t$ is conditioned on reference slices from higher hierarchical levels. This is expressed through the conditional probability $p(\hat{z}_t|\hat{z}_{\tau_t})$, where $\hat{z}_{\tau_t}$ represents the previously coded reference parameters.

![in-parameter-coding-structure](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.28163v1/NVRC++_codingstructure.png)
*{fig:in-parameter-coding-structure}: The hierarchical in-parameter coding structure organizes feature grid slices into levels ($K$), allowing the model to use temporal references within the latent space.*

Optimizing such a model is computationally expensive. Standard backpropagation through long dependency chains in a video can quickly exceed the memory capacity of even high-end GPUs. To make training feasible for long 1080p sequences, NVRC++ employs two key strategies:
*   **Unconditional Quantization:** The quantization step sizes are kept independent of previous frames, and offsets are removed. This decouples the rate estimation from the distortion computation.
*   **Random Sampling:** Because the rate and distortion calculations are decoupled, the optimizer can randomly sample frames during training. This prevents the need to load and process the entire video sequence in every iteration, enabling the training of models with over 100 million parameters on consumer-grade hardware.

Furthermore, the authors introduce **Feature Grid Masking**. At low bitrates, high-resolution grids tend to overfit to local noise rather than global structure. By randomly masking higher-resolution grids early in training and gradually annealing the masking ratio to zero, the model is forced to prioritize lower-resolution grids first, ensuring that it captures broad spatio-temporal redundancies before refining fine details.

## Entropy Modeling of High-Dimensional Latents

Once the feature grids are optimized, they must be compressed. NVRC++ utilizes an advanced entropy model that predicts the probability distribution of quantized grid parameters using three types of "priors":
1.  **Scale Priors:** High-resolution grids are conditioned on information from lower-resolution grids.
2.  **Temporal Priors:** Slices are coded sequentially, using the hierarchical structure mentioned previously.
3.  **Spatial Priors:** Within a single grid slice, parameters are coded in multiple steps (typically 1 to 4). Parts of the slice are masked and predicted based on already-coded neighbors.

The goal of the entropy model is to minimize the rate $R$. During optimization, the framework minimizes a combined loss function:
$$L = D(\hat{\theta}) + \lambda R(\hat{\theta})$$
where $D$ is the distortion (e.g., Mean Squared Error or MS-SSIM) and $\lambda$ is a Lagrange multiplier that controls the trade-off between quality and file size. By including quantization and entropy parameters $\phi$ and $\psi$ in the end-to-end optimization, the model learns the most efficient way to represent the video given a specific bitrate constraint.

## Performance Analysis: Scaling Across Complexity

NVRC++ is evaluated across four complexity scales, labeled S1 (ultra-lightweight) through S4 (high-performance). A defining characteristic of this framework is that for a given scale (e.g., S3), the decoding complexity remains constant regardless of the target bitrate. This is a significant departure from previous INR codecs where higher quality often required exponentially more computation.

![decoding-speed-vs-rate-distortion](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.28163v1/NVRC++_tradeoff1.png)
*{fig:decoding-speed-vs-rate-distortion}: Comparison of decoding speed (FPS) versus compression performance (BD-Rate). NVRC++ maintains a superior balance between speed and quality compared to other neural codecs.*

The results on standard datasets like UVG and MCL-JCV demonstrate that NVRC++ is highly competitive:
*   **High-End Performance:** The S4 variant achieves a PSNR BD-rate of $-72.79\%$ against x265 (veryslow), comparable to state-of-the-art autoencoder-based codecs like DCVC-FM, but with significantly lower inference costs.
*   **Real-Time Decoding:** The S3 variant provides a decoding speed of 73.4 FPS on a single GPU—over 7 times faster than the original NVRC—while maintaining similar compression quality.
*   **Ultra-Lightweight Capability:** The S1 variant operates at an impressive 365 FPS with a complexity of only 7.3 kMACs/pixel, outperforming traditional x265 and recent lightweight neural models like C3.

Ablation studies confirmed that every component of the framework plays a vital role. For instance, removing the temporal, spatial, or scale priors from the entropy model resulted in BD-rate increases (performance drops) of $5.13\%$, $1.42\%$, and $6.12\%$ respectively. The multi-reference input design also proved superior to standard 2D or 3D convolutional stems, providing a $7.76\%$ to $35.55\%$ improvement in BD-rate.

## Qualitative Results and Conclusion

Visually, NVRC++ produces reconstructions that preserve fine textures and sharp edges better than many existing codecs at similar bitrates. By leveraging high-resolution feature grids and a sophisticated temporal dependency model, it avoids the "blurring" artifacts often associated with low-bitrate neural compression.

![visual-comparison](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.28163v1/NVRC++_visual1.png)
*{fig:visual-comparison}: Visual comparison of NVRC++ against NVRC and DCVC-RT. NVRC++ achieves higher PSNR and MS-SSIM at a similar bits-per-pixel (bpp) rate, resulting in clearer details.*

In summary, NVRC++ represents a significant step forward for implicit neural video representation. By decoupling complexity from quality through the use of high-resolution grids and introducing a memory-efficient optimization framework, the authors have created a codec that is both highly performant and practically deployable. Whether for ultra-low-latency applications requiring hundreds of frames per second or high-fidelity archival storage, the four-scale architecture of NVRC++ provides a versatile solution that addresses the diverse needs of modern video distribution. The ability to train on long sequences and decode in real-time across multiple quality levels positions NVRC++ as a robust alternative to both traditional standards and current autoencoder-based neural codecs.
