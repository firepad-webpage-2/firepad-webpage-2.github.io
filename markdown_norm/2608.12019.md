## Galois Embedding and Trace Forms

The central problem in Galois theory is the inverse Galois problem: determining which finite groups can be realized as Galois groups of field extensions over a base field, such as the rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$. A more nuanced version of this is the Galois embedding problem, which asks if an existing Galois extension $L/K$ with Galois group $B$ can be embedded into a larger Galois extension $M/K$ with a group $G$. In this scenario, $A$ is the kernel of the mapping from $G$ to $B$, such that $G/A \cong B$.

A significant challenge within this field involves central extensions, particularly those involving the alternating group $A_n$. Specifically, mathematicians are interested in when an $A_n$-extension can be embedded into a $\tilde{A}_n$-extension, where $\tilde{A}_n$ is a non-split double cover of $A_n$. Serre’s theorem provides a fundamental criterion for solving these quadratic embedding problems. It states that for a Galois closure $\hat{E}$ of a field $E$ with $\text{Gal}(\hat{E}/\mathbb{Q}) \cong G$, a larger extension $M$ with $\text{Gal}(M/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \tilde{G}$ exists if and only if the Hasse-Witt invariant $\epsilon_p(E)$ equals 1 for every prime $p$, including the infinite prime.

The Hasse-Witt invariant is an algebraic property of the trace form associated with the root field $E$ of an irreducible polynomial $f(x)$. The trace form is a quadratic form that captures structural information about the field extension. Specifically, for the root field $E$, the trace form $Q_f: E \to E$ is defined by:
$$
\gamma \mapsto \text{tr}(\gamma^2)
$$
This form is non-degenerate and can be diagonalized into the form:
$$
\sum_{t=0}^{n-1} D_t x_t^2
$$
In this diagonalization, the coefficients $D_t$ are directly related to the principal minors $\Delta_t$ of the power-sum matrix of the polynomial's roots. Because the Hasse-Witt invariant depends on these $D_t$ values, the ability to realize specific Galois groups over $\mathbb{Q}$ depends heavily on the explicit calculation of these determinants.

## Beyond Laguerre Polynomials: The Jacobi Family

Previous research by Feit and Hajir focused on using Generalized Laguerre Polynomials (GLP) to construct extensions that satisfy Serre’s criterion. GLPs are a one-parameter family of orthogonal polynomials that have properties allowing for the explicit calculation of $\Delta_t$. Using these, it was shown that $\tilde{A}_n$ occurs as a Galois group over $\mathbb{Q}$ for specific values of $n$, such as $n \equiv 1 \pmod 8$. However, the techniques used for GLPs relied on specific three-term recursion relations and congruences that do not naturally extend to other polynomial families.

This work expands the scope by considering the two-parameter family of Jacobi Polynomials. Jacobi Polynomials include GLPs as a limiting case but also encompass a wide variety of other classical orthogonal polynomials, including Legendre, Hermite, and Chebyshev polynomials. By moving from a one-parameter to a two-parameter family, researchers gain significantly more flexibility in constructing number fields. The introduction of the second parameter ($\alpha$ and $\beta$) means that for any given degree $n$, there is a much larger search space for parameters that result in a Hasse-Witt invariant of 1 across all primes.

The challenge in this generalization lies in the fact that standard Jacobi polynomials $P_n^{(\alpha, \beta)}(x)$ are not monic and have coefficients that make direct determinant calculation difficult. To address this, the authors work with a transformed and normalized version, denoted as $\mathcal{J}_n^{(\alpha, \beta)}(x)$. This monic version is defined by shifting and scaling the standard polynomial:
$$
\mathcal{J}_n^{(\alpha, \beta)}(x) := (-1)^n \binom{2n+\alpha+\beta}{n}^{-1} J_n^{(\alpha, \beta)}(-x)
$$
where $J_n^{(\alpha, \beta)}(x) := P_n^{(\alpha, \beta)}(2x+1)$. The goal is then to find an explicit formula for the principal minors of the power-sum matrix for the roots of $\mathcal{J}_n^{(\alpha, \beta)}(x)$.

## Methodology: A Combinatorial Approach to Determinants

To calculate the principal minors $\Delta_t$, the authors shift away from the traditional reliance on the orthogonality of polynomials. Instead, they use a methodology based on Hankel determinants and the theory of generating functions. A Hankel matrix is one where the entries are constant along the anti-diagonals. The $t \times t$ principal minor of the power-sum matrix for a polynomial is a Hankel determinant:
$$
\Delta_t = \det(s_{i+j})_{0 \leq i,j \leq t-1}
$$
where $s_k = \sum_{j=1}^n \theta_j^k$ represents the $k$-th power sum of the roots $\theta_j$. The generating function for these power sums is defined as:
$$
G(z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty s_k z^k
$$
A critical insight in this research is the connection between the generating function and the reciprocal polynomial $\tilde{F}(z)$, where $G(z) = \tilde{F}'(z)/\tilde{F}(z)$. By expanding this generating function into a continued fraction, the authors can extract the coefficients $a_i$ required to calculate the determinant. The general form of this continued fraction is:
$$
G(z) = \frac{a_0}{1 + \frac{a_1 z}{1 + \cdots + \frac{a_{2n-1} z}{1}}}
$$
According to a theorem by Krattenthaler, the Hankel determinant can be computed directly from these coefficients:
$$
\det(\mu_{i+j})_{0 \leq i,j \leq t-1} = \mu_0^t (a_1 a_2)^{t-1} (a_3 a_4)^{t-2} \cdots (a_{2t-3} a_{2t-2})
$$
The authors derive explicit recurrence relations for a sequence of functions $f_i(m)$ to determine the $a_i$ values for Jacobi Polynomials. By using induction to relate $f_{2i}(n-\ell)$ and $f_{2i+1}(n-\ell)$ to $f_{2i}(n-\ell+1)$ and $f_{2i+1}(n-\ell+1)$, they obtain precise expressions for $a_i$ in terms of $n$, $\alpha$, and $\beta$.

## Explicit Results for Jacobi Polynomials

The primary result of the paper is an explicit combinatorial formula for the principal minors $\Delta_t$. For the monic Jacobi polynomial $\mathcal{J}_n^{(\alpha, \beta)}(x)$, the determinant is given by:
$$
\Delta_t = A_t B_t / C_t
$$
where the components are products defined as follows:
$$
A_t = \prod_{j=n-t+1}^n j^{j-(n-t)}
$$
$$
B_t = \prod_{j=n-t+2}^n [(\alpha+j)(\beta+j)]^{j-(n-t+1)} (\alpha+\beta+j)^{j-(n-t+2)}
$$
$$
C_t = \prod_{j=2n-2t+2}^{2n} (\alpha+\beta+j)^{j-(2n-2t+2)}
$$
From these determinants, the diagonal entries $D_t$ of the trace form are found using the relation $D_t = \Delta_{t+1}/\Delta_t$. The resulting formula for $D_t$ is:
$$
D_t = \frac{n! \prod_{j=n-t+1}^n (\alpha+j)(\beta+j)}{(n-t-1)!(\alpha+\beta+2n-2t+1) \prod_{j=2n-2t+2}^{2n} (\alpha+\beta+j)}
$$
These formulas allow for the direct evaluation of the Hasse-Witt invariant $\epsilon_p(E)$. The authors also provide the explicit values for the continued fraction coefficients $a_i$ that lead to these results. For $1 \leq i \leq n-1$:
*   $a_0 = n$
*   $a_1 = -(\alpha+n) / (\alpha+\beta+2n)$
*   $a_{2i} = -\frac{(n-i)(\beta+n-i+1)}{(\alpha+\beta+2n-2i+2)(\alpha+\beta+2n-2i+1)}$
*   $a_{2i+1} = -\frac{(\alpha+n-i)(\alpha+\beta+n-i+1)}{(\alpha+\beta+2n-2i+1)(\alpha+\beta+2n-2i)}$

These values constitute the necessary components to test any specific Jacobi polynomial for its suitability in Galois embedding problems.

## Implications and Significance

The introduction of these explicit formulas significantly impacts the study of Galois extensions. By providing a framework for Jacobi Polynomials, the research offers a systematic way to identify number fields that can be embedded into $\tilde{A}_n$-extensions. The flexibility afforded by two parameters ($ \alpha, \beta $) means that mathematicians are no longer limited to the specific cases found using Laguerre polynomials. 

For example, the authors demonstrate the application of their formulas on polynomials of degree $n=5$. They show that for $(\alpha, \beta) = (-18, 24)$, the Hasse-Witt invariant $\epsilon_p(E) = -1$ for $p \in \{3, \infty\}$, meaning the field does not admit the desired embedding. Conversely, for $(\alpha, \beta) = (-29/2, 5)$, they find $\epsilon_p(E) = 1$ for all primes, proving the existence of an $\tilde{A}_5$-extension.

Beyond the specific results for Jacobi Polynomials, the methodology developed in this paper is highly generalizable. Because it does not rely on the orthogonality of the polynomials, but rather on the properties of generating functions and continued fractions, it can be applied to other polynomial families. This provides a versatile tool for number theorists to compute trace form invariants, facilitating future investigations into the arithmetic of quadratic forms and the inverse Galois problem. This work successfully bridges combinatorial determinant evaluation with algebraic number theory, laying a foundation for discovering infinitely many number fields with prescribed Galois groups for any $n \geq 2$.
