## Introduction to SIM-Assisted UAV Networks

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have become a central component of modern wireless research due to their ability to provide on-demand connectivity. Unlike terrestrial base stations, UAVs offer high mobility and flexible 3D deployment, which allows them to establish strong line-of-sight (LoS) links with ground users. This makes them particularly effective for emergency response, temporary hotspot coverage, and improving network density in underserved areas. However, the performance of these aerial platforms is fundamentally limited by two factors: available onboard energy and the physical payload they can carry.

![sim-assisted-uav-serving-multiple-ground-users](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.13765v1/img-0.jpeg)
*{fig:sim-assisted-uav-serving-multiple-ground-users}: A system model featuring a UAV transmitter equipped with a Stacked Intelligent Metasurface (SIM) serving multiple ground users.*

To support multiple users simultaneously, UAVs typically require large antenna arrays. In traditional fully digital beamforming architectures, each antenna element is connected to a dedicated radio frequency (RF) chain. While this provides maximum control over the signal, it also leads to significant power consumption, high hardware complexity, and increased weight—all of which are detrimental to a drone's flight time and efficiency.

Stacked Intelligent Metasurfaces (SIMs) provide a solution to this hardware bottleneck. A SIM consists of multiple closely spaced layers of passive metasurface elements. By manipulating the phase of electromagnetic waves as they propagate through these layers, a SIM can perform complex signal processing directly in the electromagnetic domain. This cascaded structure, shown in {fig:sim-layer-structure} and {fig:cascaded-sim-architecture}, allows the system to approximate the performance of high-dimensional digital beamforming while using only a small number of power-intensive RF chains. This work focuses on optimizing such systems to maximize energy efficiency, ensuring that the UAV can provide high data rates without rapidly depleting its battery.

## The Energy Efficiency Objective

While much of the existing research on UAV and metasurface systems focuses on maximizing the sum rate (the total data throughput), this paper prioritizes energy efficiency (EE). EE is defined as the ratio of the total achievable rate to the total power consumed by the system, typically measured in bits per Joule. The objective function for energy efficiency $\eta$ is:

$$\eta = \frac{B R}{P_{\text{tot}}}$$

where $B$ is the system bandwidth, $R$ is the achievable sum rate, and $P_{\text{tot}}$ is the total power consumption. 

Maximizing this ratio is significantly more complex than maximizing the rate alone because the power consumption term $P_{\text{tot}}$ is not constant. It includes a hardware-aware component that accounts for the specific costs of the SIM-assisted architecture. The total power is modeled as:

$$P_{\text{tot}} = \xi P_{\text{tx}} + P_c$$

In this model, $\xi$ represents the power amplifier efficiency, $P_{\text{tx}}$ is the actual radiated transmit power, and $P_c$ is the constant circuit power. The circuit power is further broken down to reflect the hardware overhead:

$$P_c = N P_{\text{RF}} + P_{\text{BB}} + L M P_E + P_{\text{ctrl}}$$

Here, $N$ is the number of RF chains, $P_{\text{RF}}$ is the power per chain, $P_{\text{BB}}$ is the baseband processing power, $P_E$ is the power required to control each meta-atom, and $P_{\text{ctrl}}$ is the power for general control circuitry. Because a SIM uses many passive elements across $L$ layers and $M$ atoms per layer, the term $L M P_E$ becomes a critical factor in the energy trade-off.

## System Model and Wave-Domain Processing

The system considers a UAV equipped with a hybrid transceiver. The signal is first processed digitally in the baseband using a precoding matrix $V$. It then passes through $N$ RF chains into the SIM. The SIM acts as an analog beamformer, described by a cascaded matrix $\Psi$. 

The relationship for the total analog precoder $\Psi$ is:

$$\Psi = \Phi_L W_L \dots \Phi_2 W_2 \Phi_1 W_1$$

In this expression, each $\Phi_{\ell} = \text{diag}(\varphi_{\ell})$ represents the phase shifts applied by the $\ell$-th layer of the metasurface. Each element $\varphi_{\ell,m} = e^{j\theta_{\ell,m}}$ must satisfy a unit-modulus constraint, meaning the element only changes the phase $\theta_{\ell,m} \in [0, 2\pi)$ and not the amplitude of the wave. The matrices $W_{\ell}$ represent the electromagnetic propagation between the layers.

The UAV serves $K$ ground users, and the quality of the connection to each user depends on the UAV's 3D position $\mathbf{q}$. The channel model accounts for both large-scale path loss (how the signal weakens over distance) and Rician fading (which captures both the direct line-of-sight and scattered signal components). The Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR) for each user $k$, denoted as $\gamma_k(V, \{\Phi_{\ell}\}, \mathbf{q})$, is thus a complex function of the digital precoder, all SIM layer configurations, and the physical location of the drone.

## Joint Optimization Framework

The central contribution of the paper is a unified optimization framework designed to solve the non-convex EE maximization problem. The problem is challenging because the variables—the digital precoder $V$, the SIM phases $\Phi_{\ell}$, and the UAV position $\mathbf{q}$—are deeply coupled. The authors propose an alternating optimization approach that iterates through four main stages:

1. **Fractional Programming Reformulation:** To handle the ratio in the EE objective, the authors use Dinkelbach’s method. This transforms the fractional problem into a subtractive one, where the goal becomes maximizing $f(\mathbf{q}) = B R - \eta P_{\text{tot}}$ for a given efficiency estimate $\eta$.

2. **Digital Beamformer Update:** With the SIM phases and UAV position fixed, the digital precoder $V$ is optimized. The authors derive a closed-form solution for $V$ using the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions, ensuring that the total transmit power satisfies the constraint $\text{Tr}(\Psi V V^H \Psi^H) \leq P_{\text{max}}$.

3. **SIM Phase Optimization:** Optimizing the phases of multiple metasurface layers is difficult due to the unit-modulus constraint (the phase must lie on a complex unit circle). The authors treat this as a problem on a Riemannian manifold. They use a Riemannian Gradient Ascent method, which computes the gradient of the objective function and then projects it onto the manifold to ensure the phase updates remain valid. This process is performed layer by layer.

4. **3D Position Optimization:** The UAV position $\mathbf{q}$ is optimized within a feasible region $\mathbf{q} \in \mathcal{Q}$. Because the channel gain depends non-linearly on the distance and angles to the users, the authors use Successive Convex Approximation (SCA). This technique approximates the complex objective with a simpler, concave surrogate function that is easier to maximize at each step.

The algorithm is proven to improve the objective function monotonically, ensuring convergence to a stationary point. While effective, the complexity analysis shows that the SIM optimization stage is the most computationally demanding, with a complexity scaling as $L K^2 M^2$.

## Performance Analysis and Design Insights

The paper evaluates the proposed design against several benchmarks, including a fully digital (FD) system and a SIM-assisted system using a simpler Maximum Ratio Transmission (SIM-MRT) scheme. 

![convergence-behavior](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.13765v1/img-3.jpeg)
*{fig:convergence-behavior}: Convergence of the proposed energy efficiency optimization algorithm over several iterations.*

One of the most significant findings is the existence of an "optimal" transmit power for energy efficiency. As shown in {fig:energy-efficiency-vs-transmit-power}, the average EE initially increases with power but eventually reaches a peak and begins to decline. This happens because, at higher power levels, the marginal increase in the data rate is not enough to compensate for the linear increase in power consumption and the higher levels of inter-user interference.

![energy-efficiency-vs-transmit-power](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.13765v1/img-5.jpeg)
*{fig:energy-efficiency-vs-transmit-power}: Comparison of Energy Efficiency across different transmit power levels for the proposed, Fully Digital, and SIM-MRT schemes.*

Further results demonstrate the scalability benefits of SIM technology. In Figure 9, the paper shows that as the number of elements $M$ in each SIM layer increases, the EE of the fully digital system drops rapidly. This is because every new antenna in a digital system requires a new power-hungry RF chain. In contrast, the SIM-assisted system maintains a much higher and more stable EE because the metasurface elements are passive and require very little power ($P_E$) compared to an RF chain ($P_{\text{RF}}$).

![ee-vs-sim-layer-size](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.13765v1/img-9.jpeg)
*{fig:ee-vs-sim-layer-size}: The impact of increasing SIM element count $M$ on energy efficiency, highlighting the efficiency gap between hybrid and fully digital architectures.*

However, the research also highlights that "more is not always better." As shown in Figure 11, the EE actually decreases as the number of SIM layers $L$ increases. While more layers allow for more precise beamforming, each additional layer adds hardware power consumption $L M P_E$ and causes propagation losses. This indicates that for a given number of users, a small number of SIM layers (e.g., $L=1$ or $2$) is often sufficient to achieve the best energy efficiency.

## Conclusion

This work establishes a comprehensive framework for designing energy-efficient UAV communications using Stacked Intelligent Metasurfaces. By jointly optimizing the digital precoder, the multi-layer analog phases, and the UAV's 3D position, the authors demonstrate that SIM-assisted systems can significantly outperform traditional fully digital architectures in terms of energy efficiency. The findings provide critical guidance for future 6G networks, showing that the intelligent deployment of passive metasurface layers can overcome the energy and hardware limitations of aerial communication platforms.
