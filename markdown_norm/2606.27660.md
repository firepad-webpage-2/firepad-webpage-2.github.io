## The Challenge of Multi-view Vision-Language Models in Driving

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as a powerful tool for autonomous driving, offering superior reasoning and generalization capabilities compared to traditional perception-only systems. By integrating visual data with natural language instructions, these models can interpret complex driving scenes and explain decision-making processes. However, a significant bottleneck exists: the computational cost. 

Autonomous vehicles typically rely on a suite of cameras providing a $360^{\circ}$ view of the environment. Processing high-resolution images from multiple views simultaneously creates a massive sequence of visual tokens. For a standard VLM, the number of visual tokens grows linearly with the number of views, leading to substantial computational overhead and high inference latency. This latency is particularly problematic in safety-critical autonomous driving scenarios where real-time response is non-negotiable.

![motivation-for-dynamic-view-pruning](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.27660v2/x1.png)
*{fig:motivation-for-dynamic-view-pruning}: Traditional pruning methods often use fixed budgets across views. In contrast, dynamic pruning recognizes that some views (like the front camera) may be more critical for specific decisions (like braking) than others (like the rear camera).*

To mitigate this, researchers have explored token pruning—removing "unimportant" visual tokens to speed up the model. However, existing methods often treat all camera views equally or use static importance metrics. They fail to account for the fact that the importance of a specific camera view (e.g., front-left vs. back-right) changes dynamically depending on the current driving task and the specific instructions given to the model.

## Dynamic Information Requirements in VLMs

The researchers behind MVPruner conducted preliminary analyses to understand how VLMs process information across their internal layers. They observed two key trends that informed their design:

1.  **Layer-wise View Specialization:** In the earlier, shallower layers of the model, the attention is distributed broadly across all views to build a general understanding of the scene. As the information flows into deeper layers, the model becomes significantly better at identifying which specific views are relevant to the user's instruction.
2.  **Hierarchical Attention Patterns:** Text-to-vision attention starts with a high degree of entropy (spread out) in the early layers, indicating a global "structural" understanding. In later layers, the entropy decreases as the attention focuses on specific "semantic" regions that are relevant to the task at hand.

![analysis-of-model-attention-patterns](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.27660v2/x2.png)
*{fig:analysis-of-model-attention-patterns}: Analysis showing (a) how view recognition accuracy improves in deeper layers and (b) how attention entropy decreases, moving from global context to local task-relevant focus.*

These findings suggest that a one-size-fits-all pruning strategy is suboptimal. Instead, the pruning process should evolve: it should preserve a broad, diverse set of information in the early stages and then aggressively focus on task-specific details in the later stages.

## MVPruner: A Two-Stage Adaptive Framework

MVPruner introduces a two-stage pruning framework that mirrors the dynamic information requirements of the model. It adjusts both the "budget" (how many tokens to keep per view) and the "selection" (which specific tokens to keep) at different depths of the network.

![mvpruner-architecture](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.27660v2/x3.png)
*{fig:mvpruner-architecture}: The overall architecture of MVPruner, highlighting the two-stage pruning process: Stage 1 for structural summarization and Stage 2 for semantic focusing.*

### Stage 1: Shallow Layer Structural Summarization
The first stage occurs in the early layers, where the goal is to maintain a comprehensive visual context.

*   **Diversity-aware Ratio Allocation (DRA):** Instead of keeping the same number of tokens from every camera, DRA calculates a diversity score for each view. It uses a normalized cosine distance to measure how unique the tokens are within a view:
$$d_p(X_{v}^{p,i}, X_{v}^{p,j}) = 1 - \frac{X_{v}^{p,i} \cdot X_{v}^{p,j}}{\|X_{v}^{p,i}\| \cdot \|X_{v}^{p,j}\|}$$
Views with high feature diversity—meaning they contain a wider variety of visual information—are assigned a larger token budget $k_{1,p}$. This ensures that information-rich views are not prematurely over-pruned.

*   **Cross-stage Contribution-aware Token Selection (CCTS):** When choosing which tokens to keep, MVPruner looks at both the current and future utility of a token. It uses a greedy selection strategy that prioritizes tokens that are semantically unique (supporting the current layer's broad understanding) and highly relevant to the text instruction (supporting the deeper layer's specialized reasoning). The importance score is calculated as:
$Imp_{p,j} = U_{p,j} \times R_{p,j}$
where $U_{p,j}$ is a uniqueness score and $R_{p,j}$ is a task-relevance score.

### Stage 2: Deeper Layer Semantic Focusing
The second stage is applied in the intermediate layers, where the model has already aligned visual features with the text instruction.

*   **Instruction-aware Ratio Allocation (IRA):** In this stage, the model can identify which views are most important for the specific query. IRA calculates the relevance of each view by averaging the attention scores between its visual tokens and the tokens of the instruction. Views that are more relevant to the instruction get a higher retention ratio $r_{2,p}$.
*   **Instruction-guided Token Selection:** Finally, the model retains the specific tokens within those views that have the strongest attention connection to the instruction, effectively filtering out everything but the most critical task-related evidence.

## Performance and Efficiency

The researchers evaluated MVPruner across several autonomous driving benchmarks, including DriveLM, MAPLM (multi-sensor), and STSnu (video-based). The results demonstrate that MVPruner maintains high accuracy while significantly reducing computational requirements.

### Accuracy Retention
MVPruner achieved a performance retention of $99.2\%$ of the original model's accuracy while using only $25\%$ of the visual tokens. Even when pruned down to just $10\%$ of the original tokens, it maintained $98\%$ performance, outperforming other state-of-the-art pruning methods like Prune2Drive and FastV by several percentage points. On some benchmarks, like DriveLM, the pruned model actually slightly outperformed the full model, likely because the pruning process removed irrelevant visual noise that could have distracted the reasoning engine.

### Computational Gains
The efficiency improvements are substantial. By reducing the number of tokens processed by the Large Language Model (LLM) backbone, MVPruner achieved:
- Up to an **$87.3\%$ reduction in FLOPs** (Floating Point Operations).
- A **$4.97\times$ speedup** in the prefilling phase (the initial processing of the prompt).
- Significant reductions in peak GPU memory usage, making it more feasible to run these models on edge hardware found in vehicles.

## Qualitative Insights

Visualizing what MVPruner keeps versus what it discards provides clear evidence of its adaptive nature. In the first stage, the model keeps a scattered but broad set of tokens across all views. By the second stage, the tokens are highly concentrated on the specific objects or areas mentioned in the instruction.

![visualization-of-token-selection](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.27660v2/x6.png)
*{fig:visualization-of-token-selection}: A comparison showing how MVPruner focuses on the specific camera view and objects (highlighted in red) requested by the instruction, whereas static methods like Prune2Drive distribute tokens more uniformly.*

Furthermore, the model demonstrates high sensitivity to the input instruction. If the scene remains the same but the instruction changes (e.g., from asking about the front-right view to the back-left view), MVPruner reallocates the token budget to the newly relevant camera views. This dynamic adaptability is a major departure from previous methods that rely on fixed heuristics for specific views.

## Impact on the Field

MVPruner addresses one of the most practical hurdles in deploying sophisticated AI for autonomous vehicles: the trade-off between intelligence and speed. By recognizing that a model's information needs change as it "thinks" through a problem, the researchers have created a method that is both more efficient and more task-aligned than static pruning techniques. 

The significance of this work lies in its "plug-and-play" nature. It does not require re-training the base Vision-Language Model, making it a scalable solution for various existing and future architectures. As autonomous driving moves toward more interactive and reasoning-based systems, techniques like MVPruner will be essential for making these models responsive enough for real-world roads.
