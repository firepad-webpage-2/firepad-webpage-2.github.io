## Integrated Sensing, Communication, and Computing in 6G Systems

The vision for Sixth-Generation (6G) wireless networks extends far beyond traditional data transmission. Emerging applications such as extended reality (XR), autonomous driving, and immersive human-machine interaction require not only ultra-high-speed communication but also massive computational power and high-resolution environmental sensing. End devices, often constrained by battery life and physical size, frequently lack the resources to meet these demands locally.

To address these challenges, researchers have proposed Integrated Sensing, Communication, and Computing (ISCC) systems. This paradigm unifies three formerly distinct domains:
1.  **Communication**: High-bandwidth data transfer between devices and infrastructure.
2.  **Computing**: Offloading intensive tasks to Mobile Edge Computing (MEC) servers or the cloud to reduce device-side latency.
3.  **Sensing**: Utilizing wireless signals to detect and classify environmental targets, a necessity for safety in intelligent transportation.

![system-model](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.14086v1/system_model.png)

A major bottleneck in previous ISAC (Integrated Sensing and Communication) systems was the requirement to offload raw sensing data to the edge, which consumed valuable uplink bandwidth and competed with standard communication tasks. This paper addresses this by introducing a "passive sensing" approach, where the Base Station reuses the existing uplink communication signals to sense the environment, eliminating the need for dedicated sensing data transmission.

## Architecture and Passive Sensing Mechanisms

The proposed system utilizes a three-tier architecture: the device tier, the edge tier (MEC server located at the Base Station), and the cloud tier. Each device $k$ among a total of $K$ devices possesses a computation-intensive task defined by the data size $I_k$ and the required CPU cycles $c_k$. These tasks are divisible, meaning they can be partially executed locally on the device, offloaded to the MEC server, or sent further to the cloud server.

![system-flow](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.14086v1/flow.png)
*{fig:system-flow}: The logical flow of the ISCC framework, illustrating task partitioning between tiers and the dual use of signals for data decoding and target detection.*

To manage hardware costs while maintaining the beamforming precision required for high-frequency millimeter-wave (mmWave) bands, the authors employ a hybrid beamforming architecture. The Base Station is equipped with $N$ antennas but a limited number of radio-frequency (RF) chains $N_{RF}$. Similarly, devices have $M$ antennas and $M_{RF}$ RF chains. 

In this setup, the Base Station performs sensing by analyzing the echoes of the communication signals transmitted by the devices. By using a "decoding-then-sensing" framework, the Base Station first decodes the offloaded data and then uses the reconstructed signal as a reference to perform matched filtering. This allows the system to estimate the delay and Doppler shift of targets in the environment without requiring any additional spectrum or hardware overhead.

## Cloud-Edge Collaborative Inference and DNN Splitting

Beyond simple target detection, modern sensing tasks often involve intelligent classification using Deep Neural Networks (DNNs). However, executing complex DNNs entirely at the edge can overwhelm the MEC server, especially when it is simultaneously processing offloaded computation tasks from multiple devices.

The researchers introduce a split inference mechanism to alleviate this burden. The DNN used for sensing is partitioned at a splitting point $l_s$. The early layers of the network are executed at the MEC server, while the remaining layers are offloaded to the more powerful cloud server. The MEC server transmits intermediate feature representations $q(l_s)$ to the cloud. This collaborative approach creates a trade-off:
*   **Edge Processing**: Executing more layers at the edge reduces the size of intermediate features to be transmitted but increases the MEC server's computational load.
*   **Cloud Processing**: Offloading more layers to the cloud frees up edge resources but may increase transmission latency due to the larger size of early-layer features.

This division of labor ensures that both sensing intelligence and device computation tasks are handled efficiently across the multi-tier hierarchy.

## Mathematical Modeling and Resource Allocation

The primary goal of the system is to minimize the average completion latency for all device tasks. This latency is influenced by the task partitioning ratios $\alpha_k$ (local), $\beta_k$ (edge), and $\gamma_k$ (cloud), where:
$$\alpha_k + \beta_k + \gamma_k = 1$$

The total completion time for a device $k$ is the maximum of the delays across the three tiers:
$$T^{tot}_k = \max(T^L_k, T^E_k, T^C_k)$$

The system must solve a complex optimization problem:
$$\min \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K T^{tot}_k$$

This minimization is subject to several critical constraints:
1.  **Sensing Performance**: The detection probability $P_D$ must stay above a minimum threshold $P_{\min,D}$. This is mathematically enforced by ensuring the reflected signal energy meets a specific requirement:
$$T \sum_{k=1}^K \text{Tr}(A_k F_k F_k^H A_k^H) \geq \Gamma$$
2.  **Resource Limits**: The CPU frequencies at the device ($f^L_k$), edge ($f^M_k$), and cloud ($f^C_k$) are shared among all active tasks.
3.  **Beamforming Constraints**: The analog components of the hybrid beamformers must maintain constant-modulus (unit power) elements.

The integration of these constraints means that communication, sensing, and computing are deeply coupled. For example, changing the beamforming matrix $F_k$ to improve the data rate $R_k$ might inadvertently decrease the sensing energy reflected toward a target, violating the sensing constraint.

## Algorithmic Solution: Alternating Optimization

The formulated problem is highly non-convex and involves both continuous and discrete variables (like the splitting point $l_s$). To solve it, the authors developed an Alternating Optimization (AO) algorithm that decomposes the problem into three iterative subproblems:

1.  **Resource Allocation and DNN Splitting**: For a fixed beamformer, the algorithm searches for the optimal DNN split point $l_s$ and allocates CPU frequencies ($f^{M*}_k, f^{C*}_k, f^{M*}_0, f^{C*}_0$) using closed-form solutions derived from Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions.
2.  **Hybrid Beamforming Design**: The algorithm optimizes the transmit beamformers ($F_{RF,k}F_{D,k}$) and receive combiners ($V_{RF}V_{D,k}$). It uses the Weighted Minimum Mean Square Error (WMMSE) method to transform the rate-maximization problem and employs manifold optimization to handle the difficult constant-modulus constraints of the analog hardware.
3.  **Task Partitioning**: The algorithm determines the optimal values for $\alpha_k, \beta_k, \gamma_k$ by equalizing the execution times across the local, edge, and cloud tiers to ensure no single tier becomes a bottleneck.

## Performance Analysis and Practical Considerations

Numerical results confirm that the proposed framework significantly outperforms traditional benchmarks.

![convergence](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.14086v1/fig1.png)
*{fig:convergence}: Convergence of the average execution latency over iterations for different MEC capacities $f^M_{th}$ and bandwidths $B$. The algorithm typically converges within 4-5 iterations.*

One key finding is the inherent trade-off between sensing and communication. As the detection probability threshold $P^{\min}_D$ increases, the average execution latency also increases because the beamformers must shift energy away from the optimal communication directions to cover the sensing target.

![latency-vs-size](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.14086v1/fig2.png)
*{fig:latency-vs-size}: Average execution latency versus task size $I_k$. The proposed scheme consistently achieves lower latency than non-split (NSI) or fixed allocation (FCRA) schemes, approaching the performance of a system without sensing (TTWS).*

The research also explores the robustness of the system to practical impairments:
*   **Decoding Errors**: The sensing performance remains stable as long as the Bit Error Rate (BER) is below $10^{-2}$.
*   **Timing Errors**: Small synchronization errors have a limited impact, but large offsets degrade detection probability due to reference signal mismatch.
*   **Cooperative Gain**: Sensing performance improves dramatically as the number of devices $K$ increases, as the Base Station can aggregate echoes from more uplink transmissions.

## Conclusion and Future Prospects

The analyzed paper provides a comprehensive framework for integrating sensing, communication, and computing in multi-tier 6G networks. By reusing uplink signals for passive sensing and employing split DNN inference, the system maximizes resource efficiency and minimizes user latency under practical hardware constraints.

The significance of this work lies in its holistic treatment of the ISCC coupling. It moves beyond theoretical bounds to offer an algorithmic path for managing the complex interplay between beamforming, task offloading, and computational resource sharing. Future research may build on this by investigating end-to-end optimization of signal processing and DNN layers, as well as refining feature compression techniques to further reduce the overhead of cloud-edge collaborative inference.
