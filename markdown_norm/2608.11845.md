## The Challenge of 3D Communication over Lossy Networks

As applications such as autonomous driving, virtual reality (VR), and remote sensing become more prevalent, the need for efficient 3D data representation has grown significantly. Point clouds are a primary format for this data, consisting of a vast collection of points in 3D space, each defined by coordinates and often attributes like color or intensity. However, the sheer volume of data in a high-density point cloud is immense, necessitating robust Point Cloud Compression (PCC) techniques for storage and transmission.

Standard PCC methods, such as the MPEG G-PCC or more recent learning-based neural codecs, are typically designed with the assumption that the data will be transmitted over a perfect, lossless channel. In the real world, however, network conditions are often unstable. Packet loss is a common occurrence in wireless communication or congested networks. When a bitstream representing a point cloud loses a packet, traditional decoders often experience a "cliff effect," where even minor data loss leads to catastrophic reconstruction failures, such as large geometric holes or the total collapse of the 3D structure.

ResPCC introduces a paradigm shift by integrating loss resilience directly into the neural codec architecture. Instead of relying on external error correction (which adds overhead) or post-processing concealment (which is often insufficient), ResPCC adapts its encoding and decoding processes to be "packet-loss-aware." This enables the system to maintain high-fidelity reconstructions even when substantial portions of the compressed data are lost during transmission.

## Architecture of a Loss-Resilient Neural Codec

The ResPCC framework is built upon a hierarchical deep point cloud compression (DPCC) backbone. It utilizes a series of downsampling blocks to compress the input point cloud into a compact latent representation. This representation is then quantized, entropy-coded into packets, and transmitted. At the receiver, the decoder reverses these steps to reconstruct the 3D geometry.

The unique contribution of ResPCC lies in four specific modules designed to mitigate the impact of data loss: Condition-Adaptive Latent Modulation (CALM), Spatial-Channel Interleaving (SCI), Mask-Aware Graph-based Latent Restoration (MGLR), and Dictionary-based Refinement (DBR).

By distributing these modules across the encoding and decoding stages, the framework ensures that the latent features are both robustly formed and intelligently recovered if corrupted.

## Adaptive Encoding: Condition-Adaptive Latent Modulation

The first line of defense in ResPCC is the Condition-Adaptive Latent Modulation (CALM) module. Most neural codecs treat all latent features equally, but in a lossy environment, some features are more critical for structural reconstruction than others. CALM allows the encoder to adjust its behavior based on the estimated packet loss rate $p$ of the network.

When the encoder processes the point cloud, CALM takes the loss rate $p$ and transforms it into a high-dimensional embedding. This embedding informs the network in two ways:
1.  **Feature Injection:** It generates a packet-loss-aware feature $F_p \in \mathbb{R}^C$ that is added to the latent representation, providing a global context regarding the transmission risk.
2.  **Gating Mechanism:** It produces a set of gating factors $G = \{g_{anc}, g_{pos}, g_{dens}\}$ that scale the importance of different geometric attributes, such as the positions of points and their local density.

This mechanism allows the encoder to prioritize the "most resilient" features or adapt the feature distribution to be more easily recoverable by the decoder if lost.

## Scattering Corruption: Spatial-Channel Interleaving

One of the most damaging types of data loss in neural compression is channel-wise extinction. If a packet containing a specific feature channel is lost, the decoder loses an entire dimension of information for a spatial region. To prevent this, ResPCC employs Spatial-Channel Interleaving (SCI).

SCI reorders the latent data before it is packetized. First, the 3D points are ordered using a Morton (Z-order) curve, which ensures that points close together in 3D space are usually close together in the data sequence. This sequence is then reshaped into a 2D tensor $y_{2D} \in \mathbb{R}^H \times W \times C$. 

The core of SCI is a bijective permutation applied to small blocks of the data. This permutation scatters elements from the same channel to different spatial packets. Consequently, if a packet is lost, instead of losing an entire feature channel for a block of points, the decoder only loses scattered individual elements. This transformation makes the subsequent restoration task much simpler, as the decoder can use the surrounding "surviving" elements to infer the missing ones.

## Intelligent Recovery: Mask-Aware Graph Restoration

When the decoder receives the bitstream, it identifies which parts of the latent representation are missing using a binary mask. The Mask-Aware Graph-based Latent Restoration (MGLR) module is then used to fill in these gaps.

Unlike simple interpolation, MGLR uses the geometric relationship between points. It constructs a graph where each point (or anchor) is a node, and edges connect $k$-nearest neighbors. For every missing feature, MGLR calculates a restoration term $\delta_i$ by aggregating information from its neighbors. The aggregation is controlled by two key weights:
-   **Mask-aware reliability** $w_{mask}$: This weight prioritizes neighbors that have more intact (non-lost) features.
-   **Distance-aware weight** $w_{dist}$: This weight uses a Gaussian-like function to favor neighbors that are spatially closer to the missing point.

$$
w_{dist} = \exp\left(-\frac{\|a_i - a_j\|_2^2}{\sigma^2}\right)
$$

By combining these weights, MGLR can effectively "hallucinate" the missing latent values based on the most reliable and relevant local context available in the received data.

## Refining the Manifold: Dictionary-based Refinement

The final step in the restoration process is the Dictionary-based Refinement (DBR) module. While graph-based restoration is effective, it can sometimes produce "blurred" or over-smoothed features that don't perfectly match the distribution of valid point cloud data.

DBR addresses this by using a learnable codebook or dictionary $D \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times C}$ that contains prototype latent patterns. The restored features act as queries in a cross-attention mechanism that looks up the most similar valid patterns in the dictionary. The final refined feature $y^*$ is calculated by adding a weighted version of these dictionary priors to the restored features:

$$
y^* = \hat{y} + \gamma \cdot \text{Attention}(\hat{y}, D, D)
$$

where $\gamma$ is a learnable scaling factor. This process "pushes" the restored features back onto the manifold of realistic 3D geometries, sharpening the final reconstructed point cloud.

## Experimental Evaluation and Results

The researchers evaluated ResPCC on two major datasets: ShapeNet (for object-level geometry) and SemanticKITTI (for large-scale outdoor LiDAR scenes). They compared it against several baselines, including the traditional G-PCC and various state-of-the-art neural codecs like DPCC and OctAttention.

The performance was measured using Rate-Distortion (R-D) curves, which plot the trade-off between the bits per point (bitrate) and the reconstruction quality (measured by Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio, PSNR).

![rate-distortion-results](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.11845v1/mainresult_test.png)
*{fig:rate-distortion-results}: Rate-Distortion curves on ShapeNet and SemanticKITTI at different Packet Loss Rates (PLR). ResPCC (red line) consistently stays above the baselines, indicating better quality at the same bitrate.*

**Key Findings:**
-   **Intrinsic Robustness:** ResPCC consistently outperformed all other methods across all packet loss rates (from 5% to 30%). At a 30% loss rate, ResPCC achieved gains of over 14 dB in PSNR compared to standard neural codecs.
-   **Avoidance of the Cliff Effect:** While other codecs showed sharp drops in quality as soon as loss occurred, ResPCC's performance degraded gracefully, maintaining usable 3D models even under severe network congestion.
-   **Adaptive Training:** The model was trained using a mixed distribution of loss rates, where $p$ was sampled from a Beta distribution with parameters $\alpha = 1.1$ and $\beta = 2$. This training strategy ensured that a single ResPCC model could handle any loss rate between 0% and 50% without needing to be retrained.

## Significance and Real-World Impact

ResPCC represents a significant step toward making 3D communication practical for real-world networks. By moving away from the "perfect channel" assumption, it addresses the fundamental vulnerability that has hindered the deployment of neural point cloud codecs in mobile and autonomous applications.

The modular nature of the approach—specifically the use of interleaving to scatter errors and graph-based restoration to recover them—provides a blueprint for building resilience into other types of 3D data compression, such as mesh compression or neural radiance fields (NeRFs). For autonomous vehicles, this ensures that the environmental perception data remains reliable even if the vehicle's connection to a cloud server or another vehicle is intermittent. In the metaverse and VR, it translates to a more stable user experience, where network glitches no longer result in jarring visual artifacts or missing objects in the virtual world.
