The automation of scientific research has historically focused on procedural efficiency—the ability of machines to execute predefined workflows, optimize experimental parameters, and manage high-throughput data. While successful in specific domains, these systems often operate within a "scientific frame" defined entirely by humans. They lack "epistemic autonomy," or the capacity to construct, challenge, and revise the underlying physical explanations of the systems they study.

Researchers have introduced AHOIS (Autonomous High-dimensional Optical Intelligence System), a multi-agent framework designed to bridge this gap. By operationalizing the principle of "Socratic midwifery," AHOIS does not just execute experiments; it interrogates its own reasoning to ensure that its claims are grounded in verifiable physical structures. This approach allows the system to transition from simply operating instruments to actively discovering mechanistic models in complex physical environments, such as high-dimensional optical fibers.

## The Multi-Agent Architecture

The AHOIS system is composed of five specialized agents that communicate through a shared scientific state. This modularity allows for a clear separation of concerns, from high-level strategy to low-level hardware control and real-time integrity monitoring.

1.  **Boxue (Strategy Agent):** Acts as the scientific planner. It formulates hypotheses based on research goals and generates experimental plans.
2.  **Gewu (Hardware-Abstraction Agent):** Translates the machine-readable plans from Boxue into executable code for hardware components like lasers and digital micromirror devices (DMDs).
3.  **Mingde (System-Integrity Agent):** Monitors the live experimental environment. It checks for data validity, detector saturation, and hardware safety, ensuring the agent system does not reason from corrupted data.
4.  **Qiushi (Modelling Agent):** Manages the computational side of the discovery process, including data processing, neural network training, and quantitative analysis.
5.  **Duzhi (Socratic Physics Critic):** This agent is central to the system's epistemic autonomy. Rather than suggesting solutions, Duzhi interrogates Boxue’s reasoning. It identifies hidden assumptions, checks for physical consistency, and demands counterexamples to ensure the proposed scientific models are robust.

By separating the "proposer" (Boxue) from the "critic" (Duzhi), the system mimics a scientific peer-review process in real-time. This internal dialogue, or Socratic midwifery, forces the system to refine its internal models before and after experimental execution.

## High-Dimensional Physics in Multimode Fibers

The chosen testbed for AHOIS is a multimode-fiber (MMF) optical platform. MMFs are inherently complex because light propagating through them undergoes extensive mode mixing and interference. This results in a "speckle" pattern—a high-dimensional, seemingly random intensity distribution.

In such a system, the relationship between the input wavefront and the output signal is extremely sensitive to environmental conditions and fiber geometry. For an AI agent, this environment presents significant "epistemic" challenges: Does a change in the output signal represent a useful physical encoding of an object, or is it merely noise, decorrelation, or hardware drift? Distinguishing between these possibilities requires a deep understanding of optical physics, making it an ideal environment to test whether an AI can move beyond simple pattern matching.

## Autonomous Discovery of Speckle Encoding

One of the primary demonstrations of AHOIS involved the discovery of "backscattered speckle encoding." Traditionally, coherent backscattering in optical fibers is viewed as an imaging artifact or noise. However, AHOIS was tasked with investigating whether this backscattered light could be used as a signal.

Boxue proposed that object-dependent backscattering could function as a high-dimensional random-interference encoder. Duzhi challenged this hypothesis by requiring Boxue to define quantitative criteria for a "good" encoder: low redundancy, high dimensionality, and sufficient dynamic range.

To validate this, the system performed experiments where it scanned a laser focus across targets and collected the backscattered light into $16 \times 16$ encoder matrices. The analysis by Qiushi confirmed that these matrices occupied a multidimensional feature space with an effective rank of $R_{\text{eff}} = 56.9$. A classifier trained on this data achieved accuracies of $76.97\%$ for MNIST and $83.17\%$ for Fashion-MNIST, demonstrating that the agent had successfully identified and characterized a previously unused physical mechanism for information retrieval.

## Adaptive Sparse Scanning Strategies

In another instance, AHOIS addressed the inefficiency of traditional raster scanning. Standard imaging systems often sample the entire field of view uniformly, which is time-consuming and often redundant.

AHOIS proposed a "coarse-to-fine" adaptive scanning policy. Duzhi's interrogation refined this idea into a structured protocol:
1.  Perform an ultra-sparse background scan to identify regions of interest (ROIs).
2.  Densely sample only those ROIs.
3.  Implement a stopping criterion based on the plateauing of reconstruction quality.

The results showed that this adaptive strategy could achieve task-relevant recognition at video rates. For example, in a biological endoscopy task, the ROI-adapted scan reached $48.50$ frames per second, compared to just $0.47$ frames per second for a full raster scan. This demonstrates how physical understanding can lead to significant gains in experimental efficiency.

## Failure Diagnosis and Protocol Reproduction

A critical test of epistemic autonomy is the ability to handle unexpected results. AHOIS demonstrated "modality-specific diagnosis" when moving between far-field imaging (dominated by speckle noise) and fluorescence imaging (dominated by detector noise).

When imaging quality degraded, the Mingde agent flagged the integrity issue. Boxue and Duzhi then reasoned about the underlying cause based on the imaging modality. They correctly identified that the far-field system required speckle-suppression algorithms, while the fluorescence system required denoising filters. The Gewu agent then autonomously invoked the appropriate computational tools (SCNet or MFNet) to resolve the issue.

Furthermore, the system showed it could translate scientific knowledge from existing literature into local experiments. Given a PDF of a published MMF imaging paper, AHOIS extracted the core methodology, accounted for differences in local hardware, and autonomously established a pilot acquisition and training pipeline. This "paper-to-platform" capability suggests that Socratic agents can assist in the global reproducibility of scientific results.

## Quantifying the Socratic Advantage

The authors conducted ablation studies to determine if the "Socratic" interrogation actually improved discovery outcomes. They compared a "Boxue only" configuration against versions with a generic reviewer, a non-Socratic critic, and the full Socratic Duzhi.

![evaluation-of-the-socratic-method](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.26722v1/x5.png)
*{fig:evaluation-of-the-socratic-method}: Evaluation of Socratic midwifery. (a-b) Improvement in physics consistency and hypothesis completeness over rounds of questioning. (c) Causal probing ($0.83$) and counterexample challenges ($0.74$) were found to be the most effective interrogation types. (d) Comparison of AHOIS against other agent configurations.*

The study used several levels of questioning:
*   **Clarification:** Defining physical terms.
*   **Constraint-Checking:** Ensuring compatibility with hardware limits.
*   **Causal-Probing:** Linking modulation to measurement through a complete causal chain.
*   **Counterexample Challenge:** Testing the hypothesis under different conditions.

Quantitative analysis showed that the Socratic configuration produced the highest scores in physics consistency and plan validity. Specifically, causal probing (utility gain of $0.83$) and counterexample challenges (utility gain of $0.74$) were the most effective at refining the agent's physical understanding. Without these interventions, the agents were more likely to propose plans that were physically inconsistent or failed to account for hardware limitations like coherence or efficiency $\eta$.

## Significance and Future Directions

The AHOIS framework demonstrates that multi-agent systems can achieve a degree of epistemic autonomy by incorporating self-interrogation. By moving beyond procedural execution, these agents can manage the ambiguity inherent in high-dimensional physical systems.

While currently limited by natural-language-based reasoning and the need for human safety gates, the success of AHOIS in a complex optical environment suggests broader applicability. Similar principles could be applied to autonomous materials synthesis, adaptive microscopy, or photonic processor design—fields where the interpretation of data is as critical as the collection of it. The shift from "AI as a tool" to "AI as a Socratic collaborator" represents a new path toward trustworthy, evidence-grounded autonomous science.
