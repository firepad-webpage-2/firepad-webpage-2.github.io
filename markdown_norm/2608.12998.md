## The Need for Joint Probabilistic Forecasting

In fields like finance, supply chain management, and capacity monitoring, making decisions based on a single "most likely" outcome is often insufficient. For instance, a logistics manager needs to know not just the expected demand for a product, but also the risk of a sudden spike that could deplete inventory. More importantly, when managing multiple related items, the manager must understand how those items move together. If two products always peak at the same time, the strain on the warehouse is much greater than if their peaks were independent or offset.

Traditional forecasting often focuses on point estimates or individual uncertainty for single time series. However, real-world systems consist of multiple interdependent series. Understanding this interdependence—the "joint predictive distribution"—is critical for risk analysis and stress testing. The research paper "WIRED: Weighted Adaptive Prediction with Structured Dependence for Probabilistic Multiseries Forecasting" introduces a modular framework designed to address this challenge. WIRED (Weighted Adaptive Prediction with Structured Dependence) provides a structured approach to generating coherent future scenarios that respect both the unique behavior of individual series and the complex relationships between them.

## The Modular Architecture of WIRED

The core philosophy behind WIRED is modularity. Rather than building a single, opaque model to handle everything at once, the algorithm breaks the forecasting problem into distinct layers. This separation allows for better interpretability and makes it easier to refine specific components without redesigning the entire system.

The WIRED architecture follows a four-step sequence:
1.  **Forecast Experts:** A library of diverse models generates individual predictions for each series.
2.  **Marginal Mixture:** These individual predictions are combined into a single "marginal" predictive distribution for each series using an adaptive weighting system.
3.  **Adaptive Copula:** A specialized statistical structure called a "copula" is used to model the dependencies and correlations between the different series.
4.  **Joint Scenarios:** By combining the marginal distributions with the copula, the system generates a set of coherent, simultaneous future scenarios for all series.

This structure is designed to handle $p$-dimensional positive level processes, denoted as $y_t$, over a future horizon $h$. Before forecasting, the data is typically transformed into movements $x_{t+h,j}$, which can be additive ($y_{t+h,j} - y_{t,j}$), multiplicative ($y_{t+h,j} / y_{t,j} - 1$), or log-multiplicative ($\log(y_{t+h,j} / y_{t,j})$). The ultimate goal is to estimate the joint distribution $F_h(x_1, \dots, x_p | y_{1:T})$ that captures the likely future state of the entire system.

## Marginal Modeling: A Committee of Experts

For every individual time series in the dataset, WIRED employs a "committee" of eight different forecasting experts. Each expert has its own way of looking at the data, ranging from classic statistical models to simple historical bootstrapping. By using a diverse set of experts, the system aims to be more robust than any single model. The experts included in the library are:

*   **Naive PERT:** A simple distribution based on the most recent move.
*   **Auto-ARIMA:** An automated version of the classic AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average model.
*   **EWMA Gaussian:** An exponentially weighted moving average that assumes a bell-curve (Gaussian) distribution.
*   **Historical Bootstrap:** Randomly sampling from past movements.
*   **Drift Bootstrap:** Sampling from past movements while accounting for a persistent trend (drift).
*   **Volatility-Scaled Naive:** A Gaussian forecaster that adjusts its spread based on recent volatility.
*   **Robust Median/MAD:** A forecaster focusing on the median and the median absolute deviation, assuming either a Laplace or Gaussian shape.
*   **Shrunk Quantile Regression:** A model that estimates specific percentiles of the future distribution while applying shrinkage to prevent overfitting.

To combine these experts, WIRED calculates an adaptive weight for each one based on its recent performance. This performance is measured using the Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS), a standard metric for evaluating probabilistic forecasts. The algorithm extrapolates the expected future CRPS ($s_k$ for each expert $k$) and uses a softmax function to determine the final weights:

$$w_k = \frac{\exp(-s_k/\tau)}{\sum_{l=1}^K \exp(-s_l/\tau)}$$

Here, $\tau$ is a "temperature" parameter that controls how aggressively the system favors the top-performing expert. This weighted mixture forms the marginal predictive distribution for a single series.

## Modeling Dependence via Copulas

While the marginal mixture tells us about each series individually, it doesn't say how they move together. To bridge this gap, WIRED uses copulas. In multivariate statistics, a copula is a function that links marginal distributions together to form a joint distribution. This allows WIRED to model the "shape" of the relationship between series separately from the series themselves.

The algorithm estimates a correlation matrix $\hat{R}$ from historical data using various metrics like Pearson, Kendall, or Spearman rank correlation. Because correlations can change over time (e.g., markets becoming more correlated during a crash), WIRED offers several adaptation modes:
*   **Static:** One matrix calculated from all available history.
*   **Rolling:** Using only the most recent window of data.
*   **EWMA:** Giving more weight to recent correlations using exponential decay.
*   **Regime-Adaptive:** Blending two matrices—one for "calm" periods ($R_{\text{calm}}$) and one for "stress" periods ($R_{\text{stress}}$)—based on a calculated stress score.

To ensure the resulting matrix is mathematically valid for simulation, a shrinkage step is applied:

$$R_{\alpha} = (1 - \alpha)\hat{R} + \alpha I_p$$

where $\alpha$ is a small shrinkage factor and $I_p$ is the identity matrix. This process ensures the matrix is positive definite, allowing for stable sampling from Gaussian or Student t copulas.

## Generating Coherent Scenarios

The final stage of the WIRED process is the generation of scenarios. This is done through a process called "inverse transform sampling." 

First, the system draws a set of correlated values from the chosen copula. These values are initially uniform (between 0 and 1) but carry the desired correlation structure. Second, these uniform values are passed through the quantile functions of the marginal mixture distributions for each specific series. Finally, the predicted movements are transformed back into the original scale of the data (e.g., price or volume).

The result is a set of "joint draws." Each draw represents a possible future where all series are simulated simultaneously. If the copula captured a strong positive correlation between Series A and Series B, then in most scenarios where Series A goes up, Series B will also go up. These coherent scenarios are the primary output used for risk management and planning.

## Empirical Evaluation and Findings

The author evaluated WIRED using both synthetic datasets and real-world financial data (the EuStockMarkets dataset). The goal was to see how the "full" WIRED model compared to versions where certain parts were removed (ablation studies) and to common baseline models.

The study used several scoring rules to measure quality. The **Energy Score (ES)** and **Variogram Score (VS)** were used to evaluate the entire joint distribution, while CRPS was used for individual marginals.

Key findings from the experiments include:
1.  **The Value of Dependence Modeling:** The experiments clearly showed that including the copula layer improved the Variogram Score compared to simply sampling each series independently. This confirms that the structured dependence component is effective at reconstructing how the series relate to one another.
2.  **The Challenge of Weighting:** Interestingly, the adaptive weighting mechanism (the softmax of CRPS) did not always beat simpler methods like giving every expert equal weight. The research found that a simple Gaussian-copula bootstrap often performed better than the full adaptive mixture.
3.  **No Single "Best" Correlation Mode:** While the regime-adaptive and rolling correlation modes provided flexibility, they didn't consistently outperform the static correlation mode across all synthetic scenarios, suggesting that the benefits of adaptation depend heavily on the specific nature of the data.

## Significance and Future Directions

The significance of the WIRED paper lies in its transparent, modular framework for a complex problem. By breaking down the forecasting process, the author provides a roadmap for how multivariate probabilistic systems can be built and, crucially, how they can be diagnosed.

The finding that the adaptive weighting layer was a performance bottleneck is a valuable contribution. It suggests that while "expert committees" are a good idea, the specific way we predict which expert will be best in the next period is highly sensitive to noise. This points toward future research in more robust weighting schemes or the inclusion of stronger baseline models directly into the mixture.

For practitioners, WIRED is available as an R package on CRAN, providing a ready-to-use tool for generating probabilistic scenarios. Its design ensures that even if one part of the system (like a specific expert model) is updated, the overall logic of marginal aggregation and dependence reconstruction remains a solid foundation for multivariate time series analysis.
