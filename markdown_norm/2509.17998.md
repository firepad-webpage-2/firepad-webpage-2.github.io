## Introduction

Bayesian Optimization (BO) has become a cornerstone methodology for optimizing expensive, black-box objective functions where gradient information is unavailable. From hyperparameter tuning in machine learning to robotic controller design and scientific discovery, BO provides a principled approach to finding optimal solutions with limited function evaluations. At the heart of BO lies a Gaussian Process (GP) surrogate model, whose performance critically depends on the choice of kernel function. The kernel encodes assumptions about the objective function's smoothness, periodicity, and other statistical properties, directly influencing the balance between exploration and exploitation.

Starting with an initial population of kernels (SE, PER, LIN, RQ, M3, M5), the LLM performs crossover and mutation operations to evolve the kernel population. The process includes mating selection, LLM-guided genetic operations, fitness evaluation using BIC, and survivor selection to maintain the best-performing kernels.*

Traditional BO approaches rely heavily on fixed, general-purpose kernels or simple heuristic selection strategies. This "one-size-fits-all" approach can lead to suboptimal performance when the chosen kernel's assumptions mismatch the true characteristics of the objective function. While the field has seen significant advances in acquisition function design, the adaptive design of the surrogate model itself has received comparatively less attention.

This paper introduces **Context-Aware Kernel Evolution (CAKE)**, a method that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) as intelligent genetic operators to adaptively evolve kernel structures throughout the optimization process. By combining LLMs' reasoning capabilities with a genetic algorithm framework, CAKE dynamically constructs and refines expressive kernel structures based on observed data, moving beyond static kernel choices toward a more flexible and context-aware approach.

## Methodology

The CAKE framework operates as an evolutionary algorithm where LLMs serve as intelligent crossover and mutation operators for kernel evolution. The methodology consists of two main components: the kernel evolution process and a novel kernel selection strategy called BIC-Acquisition Kernel Ranking (BAKER).

### Context-Aware Kernel Evolution

The evolution process begins with an initial population of kernels constructed from base components: Squared Exponential (SE), Periodic (PER), Linear (LIN), Rational Quadratic (RQ), Matérn-3/2 (M3), and Matérn-5/2 (M5). These kernels can be combined using addition (+) and multiplication (×) operators following established kernel grammar rules.

At each iteration, the LLM receives few-shot examples of the observed data and is prompted to act as an expert in machine learning and Gaussian Processes. The system prompt explicitly requests chain-of-thought reasoning to improve LLM performance and provide interpretability. Two genetic operations guide the evolution:

**Crossover Operations**: For each of $n_c$ crossover operations, two parent kernels are sampled from the current population with probability proportional to their fitness. The LLM then proposes a new "child" kernel by combining the parents using specified operators, explaining its rationale for the combination.

**Mutation Operations**: With probability $p_m$, a mutation occurs where the fittest kernel from the current population is selected. The LLM proposes a new kernel by modifying one of its base kernel components, such as replacing SE with RQ, while providing reasoning for the modification.

Each proposed kernel's fitness is evaluated using the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC):

$$
\text{BIC} = -2 \log L(\theta | D) + k \log n
$$

where $L(\theta | D)$ is the likelihood of the data given the kernel parameters, $k$ is the number of parameters, and $n$ is the number of observations. BIC balances model fit with complexity, serving as an approximation of the marginal likelihood. Fitness scores are normalized to [0, 1].

### BIC-Acquisition Kernel Ranking (BAKER)

A key insight is that the kernel providing the best model fit might not necessarily yield the most impactful next query point. BAKER addresses this by selecting kernels that balance statistical model fit with acquisition function utility.

For each kernel $k$ in the current population, a weight $w_k$ is assigned based on its BIC value, where lower BIC indicates better fit and corresponds to higher weight. The acquisition function (Expected Improvement, normalized to [0, 1]) determines the utility $\alpha(x_{t,k}; D, k)$ of the candidate query point $x_{t,k}$ proposed by each kernel.

BAKER selects the kernel that maximizes:

$$
k^* = \arg\max_k (w_k \cdot \alpha(x_{t,k}; D, k))
$$

This approach ensures that the chosen kernel balances both statistical adequacy and optimization potential.

## Experimental Evaluation

The authors evaluate CAKE across diverse real-world optimization tasks, comparing against established baselines including fixed kernels (SE, Matérn-5/2), adaptive methods (Random, Utility, BIC selection), ensemble approaches (Deep GP, Ensemble GP), and compositional methods (CKS, ABO).

### Hyperparameter Optimization

CAKE was tested on 60 hyperparameter optimization tasks covering 12 OpenML datasets and 5 machine learning models (Logistic Regression, SVM, Random Forest, XGBoost, MLP). The results show CAKE consistently achieving the highest average test accuracy across all model types.

Particularly notable is CAKE's performance in early optimization stages, where it demonstrates rapid convergence to high-performing configurations. Analysis reveals that CAKE achieves 67.5% of its total improvement within just 25% of the evaluation budget, indicating highly efficient sample utilization.

### Dynamic Control Tasks

In robotic controller tuning experiments involving robot pushing and lunar lander tasks, CAKE demonstrated superior performance in dynamic environments. For the robot pushing problem, CAKE converged significantly faster to high-performing solutions, while in the lunar lander task, it achieved higher average rewards with greater consistency compared to baselines.

The results highlight CAKE's ability to adapt to environmental shifts and maintain robust performance across different dynamic scenarios, where fixed-kernel methods often plateaued or struggled to maintain high performance.

### Multi-Objective Photonic Chip Design

Perhaps most impressive is CAKE's performance in the complex multi-objective photonic chip design problem. CAKE achieved the highest scores and hypervolume, indicating superior optimization performance and better exploration of the Pareto front.

Critically, CAKE found high-scoring solutions in fewer than 40 trials, representing a tenfold speedup in the design cycle compared to baselines. This dramatic improvement translates to significant reductions in time and cost for real-world engineering applications, attributed to CAKE's ability to tailor kernels to individual objectives in the multi-objective setting.

## Key Contributions and Significance

### Adaptive Kernel Design for Bayesian Optimization

CAKE addresses a fundamental limitation in BO by moving beyond fixed or heuristic kernel choices toward dynamic, context-aware kernel design. This represents a significant advancement in surrogate modeling for BO, potentially unlocking better performance for optimization tasks where objective function properties are complex, unknown, or dynamically changing.

### LLMs as Intelligent Genetic Operators

The use of LLMs as genetic operators represents a paradigm shift in evolutionary computation. Rather than relying on random or simple heuristic operations, CAKE leverages LLMs' reasoning capabilities, pattern recognition from limited data, and generative abilities to guide kernel evolution intelligently. This opens new avenues for LLM-guided scientific optimization and discovery.

### Sample Efficiency and Interpretability

CAKE consistently demonstrates superior sample efficiency across diverse tasks, particularly excelling in early optimization stages where function evaluations are most precious. Additionally, the method provides interpretable kernel expressions with natural language explanations from the LLM, offering valuable insights into complex black-box functions.

### Broad Applicability

While demonstrated in BO contexts, the core principle of LLM-guided kernel evolution is transferable to other kernel-based machine learning tasks. The authors explicitly state a long-term goal of developing a "universal adaptive kernel method" applicable across the ML ecosystem, suggesting significant potential for broader impact.

## Implications and Future Directions

CAKE represents more than just an improvement in Bayesian Optimization; it demonstrates the transformative potential of integrating LLMs into scientific optimization workflows. The method's success in complex engineering applications like photonic chip design illustrates its capacity to accelerate scientific discovery and engineering innovation.

The interpretability aspect is particularly significant, as CAKE provides not just optimized solutions but also explanations of the underlying patterns and function characteristics. This transparency is crucial for scientific applications where understanding the "why" behind a solution is as important as finding the solution itself.

Looking forward, the framework opens several research directions: extending to other kernel-based methods, exploring different LLM architectures and prompting strategies, and investigating applications in automated scientific discovery. The consistent performance improvements across diverse domains suggest that CAKE could become a standard tool for practitioners facing expensive optimization problems where traditional methods struggle with kernel selection.
