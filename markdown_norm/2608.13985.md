## Introduction to Neuromorphic Integrated Sensing and Communications

The demand for high-efficiency wireless systems has led to the exploration of Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC), a paradigm where the same radio frequency hardware and spectrum are used for both data transmission and environmental sensing. However, implementing ISAC on resource-constrained edge devices, such as IoT sensors or mobile drones, requires extreme energy efficiency. Neuromorphic computing, inspired by the sparse and event-driven operation of the human brain, offers a promising solution through Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs).

This work investigates a standard-compliant Neuromorphic ISAC (N-ISAC) system that utilizes Impulse Radio Ultra-Wideband (IR-UWB) signaling. IR-UWB is inherently sparse in the time domain, consisting of very short pulses, which naturally aligns with the spike-based processing of SNNs. To enhance performance in challenging environments, the system incorporates a Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS), which shapes the wireless propagation to improve both communication reliability and target illumination. Unlike previous studies that relied on simplified models, this research adopts the IEEE 802.15.4z HRP UWB standard and realistic, frequency-selective channel models.

## Synergy Between SNNs and Ultra-Wideband Signaling

The fundamental motivation for N-ISAC lies in the architectural alignment between IR-UWB and SNNs. In conventional digital communications, signals are often processed as dense blocks of complex numbers. In contrast, IR-UWB transmits information using narrow pulses separated by relatively long quiet periods. SNNs operate similarly; they process information only when "spikes" occur, maintaining low power consumption during idle periods.

In this system, the receiver employs a discrete-time Spike Response Model (SRM), which is a specific type of Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron. The membrane potential of a neuron at time $t$ reflects the integration of incoming signals. When this potential exceeds a specific threshold, the neuron fires a spike. This event-driven nature allows the receiver to perform joint data demodulation and target detection with significantly lower computational energy compared to traditional Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) or classical signal processing algorithms.

## Standard-Compliant Waveform Design

To ensure practical relevance, the research adopts the IEEE 802.15.4z high-rate-pulse-repetition-frequency (HRP) UWB physical layer. This standard is widely used for secure ranging and high-speed short-range communications. The transmitted frame consists of a Synchronization Header (SHR) and a PHY payload.

![uwb-frame-structure](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.13985v1/frame_structure.png)
*{fig:uwb-frame-structure}: The standard-compliant frame structure including the SYNC field for preamble processing, the SFD for frame timing, and the PHY payload for data and sensing.*

The SHR contains $M_p^{sync}$ preamble symbols used for synchronization and channel estimation. The payload utilizes Burst-Position Modulation (BPM) combined with Binary Phase-Shift Keying (BPSK). Specifically, a symbol is divided into intervals, and the position and phase of the pulse burst within these intervals carry the data bits. This complex modulation scheme presents a significant challenge for SNN-based receivers, which must decode these multi-bit symbols while simultaneously detecting the presence of a radar target.

## RIS-Aided Wideband Channels and Frequency Selectivity

A Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS) consists of an array of metamaterial elements that can change the phase and amplitude of incident waves. While RIS is often modeled as a simple phase shifter in narrowband systems, wideband signals like UWB require a more sophisticated treatment due to frequency selectivity.

The metamaterials in an RIS have a physical response that varies across the wide bandwidth of a UWB pulse. This is characterized by a quality factor $Q$ and a damping factor $\kappa$. A high $Q$ indicates a very selective, narrow-band response, which can cause significant temporal dispersion, effectively "stretching" the UWB pulses in time. This distortion can degrade communication by causing inter-symbol interference, but as the research finds, it can also aid sensing by filtering out unwanted background clutter.

The channel model used in this work is site-specific and generated via ray tracing, accounting for:
1.  **Background Channel ($h_{bg}(t)$):** Includes static reflections from buildings and RIS-assisted paths.
2.  **Target Channel ($h_{tar}(t)$):** Includes echoes reflected off a potential passive target.

The total received signal is the convolution of the transmitted waveform $p(t)$ with the composite channel, plus noise:

$$
y_t = \sum_{\tau} h(\tau) x(t-\tau) + n(t)
$$

## The HNet SNN Receiver Architecture

The proposed HNet SNN receiver introduces a preamble-driven adaptation mechanism. Rather than performing explicit and computationally expensive channel estimation (like Least-Squares or LMMSE), the receiver uses a "hypernetwork."

The hypernetwork is a smaller neural network that takes the synchronized SYNC observations as input. It generates neuron-specific scaling coefficients that adapt the main SNN's weights to the current channel conditions. This allows the SNN to remain "channel-aware" without the need for traditional equalization blocks. 

To further save energy, the receiver employs chip-domain sparse encoding. UWB signals are sampled at a very high rate, resulting in a large number of samples (chips). The receiver only retains a fraction $\rho$ of the highest-power chips, setting the rest to zero. This input sparsification reduces the number of operations the SNN must perform, directly lowering energy consumption.

## Joint Communication and Sensing Inference

The SNN processes the sparsified payload symbols sequentially. It has two distinct readout mechanisms:
*   **Communication Readout:** The membrane potentials of the output neurons are mapped to Log-Likelihood Ratios (LLRs) for the coded bits. These LLRs are then fed into a standard-aligned Forward Error Correction (FEC) decoder (e.g., Viterbi decoder) to recover the information bits.
*   **Sensing Readout:** A separate output provides a probability estimate for the presence of a target. The receiver averages these estimates across all symbols in a frame to make a final binary decision on target presence.

The training of this network is "energy-aware." The loss function includes a sparsity-promoting regularizer $\lambda_r$ that penalizes high firing rates in the hidden layers. This encourages the network to find solutions that use as few spikes as possible to achieve the task.

## Analyzing the Communication-Sensing Trade-off

One of the key findings of the research is the complex relationship between the RIS properties and the two ISAC tasks. The frequency selectivity of the RIS, governed by the quality factor $Q$, creates a clear trade-off.

*   **Communication Performance:** Lower $Q$ values (wider bandwidth) are generally better for communication because they minimize pulse distortion and dispersion. As $Q$ increases, the RIS-assisted signal becomes more distorted, leading to higher Block Error Rates (BLER).
*   **Sensing Performance:** Interestingly, higher $Q$ values can improve target detection. A highly selective RIS acts as a filter that can suppress target-unrelated background signals, making the subtle changes caused by a target more apparent to the SNN.

## Energy Efficiency and Hardware Feasibility

The primary advantage of the N-ISAC approach is its energy efficiency. By avoiding explicit channel estimation and using spike-based processing, the HNet SNN achieves computation energy levels below 91 μJ per frame. In comparison, traditional receivers using LMMSE estimation require over 4,200 μJ per frame—a reduction of nearly two orders of magnitude.

![energy-performance-trade-off](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.13985v1/energy_performance_tradeoff_figure.png)
*{fig:energy-performance-trade-off}: Comparison of Block Error Rate and target detection error against total computation energy. HNet SNN (in blue) occupies the high-performance, low-energy region of the design space.*

Furthermore, the research demonstrates that the hidden layer firing rate $R_s(1)$ can be significantly reduced using the regularizer $\lambda_r$. For instance, increasing $\lambda_r$ can reduce the firing rate by up to 74% with only a marginal impact on communication reliability. This confirm that SNNs can be tuned to operate at the absolute minimum energy required for a specific level of performance.

## Conclusions and Future Impact

This work demonstrates that neuromorphic computing is a viable and highly efficient candidate for next-generation ISAC systems. By adhering to the IEEE 802.15.4z standard and accounting for the physics of frequency-selective RIS, the study provides a realistic assessment of N-ISAC performance. 

The proposed HNet SNN receiver offers a significant leap in energy efficiency for edge devices, enabling them to perform complex sensing and communication tasks that were previously too power-hungry. The findings regarding the communication-sensing trade-off in RIS-aided environments provide crucial design insights for future wideband wireless networks. As AI-native receiver architectures continue to evolve, the integration of neuromorphic principles will likely play a central role in achieving the low-latency, low-power requirements of the future wireless landscape.
