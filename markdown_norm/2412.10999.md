## Bridging the Gap in Human-Agent Collaboration

As large language models (LLMs) advance, AI agents are increasingly capable of performing complex, multi-step tasks. In domains such as scientific research, these agents can help discover literature, synthesize information, and plan experiments. However, the current paradigm for interacting with these agents often falls into two extremes: either the agent is fully autonomous, leaving the human as a passive observer, or the human is burdened with managing every small detail of the agent's actions.

![an-overview-of-cocoa-s-interleaved-co-planning-and-co-execution-workflow](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2412.10999v4/x1.png)

This research presents **Cocoa** (Co-Planning and Co-Execution), a system designed to facilitate deeper, more flexible collaboration between humans and AI agents. Cocoa is built on the premise that complex tasks require a fluid transition between planning a project and executing its individual steps. By integrating AI agents directly into a document environment, Cocoa allows users to delegate specific tasks to the AI while retaining control over strategic decisions. This "interleaved" approach ensures that plans can be adapted in real-time as new information is discovered during execution.

## The Challenge of Flexible Agency

Current AI agent workflows typically follow a "prescribed" structure. In agent-guided systems, the AI dictates the process, and the user only intervenes to fix errors. In user-guided systems, the human does all the planning and only hands off small, scoped subtasks. This lack of flexibility is a significant hurdle in knowledge-intensive fields like research, where the optimal division of labor between human and AI can change depending on the specific step of a project or the researcher's own expertise.

A formative study conducted with nine scientific researchers highlighted several key requirements for a collaborative research tool:
1.  **Project Documents as Hubs:** Researchers centralize their thoughts, plans, and literature in documents. They want AI assistance within these existing spaces rather than in a separate chat window.
2.  **Literature as a Foundation:** Much of the multi-step planning in research is driven by finding and understanding existing literature.
3.  **Human Control over Reasoning:** While AI is helpful for information retrieval and brainstorming, researchers prefer to handle high-level reasoning and consequential decision-making themselves to maintain ownership and ensure accuracy.

These findings suggest that a collaborative system should not just "execute" a prompt, but should support a shared journey where the human and agent can dynamically decide who handles which task.

## The "Agent Notebook" Design Pattern

Cocoa addresses these needs by introducing the "agent notebook" design pattern. Inspired by computational notebooks like Jupyter, which interleave code and its output, Cocoa allows for the interleaving of plan steps and their resulting outputs.

![the-cocoa-interface-integrated-into-a-document-environment-showing-the-plan-steps-a-agent-researcher-toggles-b-c-and-interactive-sidebar-d-e](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2412.10999v4/x2.png)

In Cocoa, when a user highlights a research question in their document, the agent proposes a structured, interactive plan. This plan is composed of several "cells" or steps. The system incorporates several unique interaction affordances:

*   **Flexible Delegation:** Each step in the plan features a toggle. The user can assign a step to the AI (represented by a robot icon) or to themselves (represented by a person icon). This allows the researcher to delegate routine tasks, like searching for papers, while personally handling critical tasks, like reasoning about a paper's findings.
*   **Stepwise vs. Continuous Execution:** Users can choose to let the agent run through several steps at once or force it to pause after each step. This pause provides an opportunity for verification and steering.
*   **Interactive Output Editing:** When an agent completes a step—such as identifying relevant papers—the results appear in a sidebar. The user can then manually refine these results (e.g., deleting irrelevant papers or adding missed ones) before the agent moves on to the next step.
*   **Dynamic Replanning:** If a user modifies a step in the middle of a plan, the agent can automatically rewrite the remaining steps to align with the new direction.

## Evaluating Steerability and Ease of Use

To evaluate the effectiveness of this interleaved approach, the authors conducted a lab study with 16 researchers, comparing Cocoa to a standard chat-based AI assistant (the baseline). The study focused on "steerability"—the ability of the user to guide the AI toward a desired outcome—and general utility.

The quantitative results showed that Cocoa significantly improved the user's sense of control. Cocoa was rated higher for steerability, with a median score of $M_c = 4$ compared to the baseline's $M_b = 3$ ($p = 0.005$). Importantly, this increase in control did not make the system harder to use; there was no significant difference in perceived ease of use between Cocoa ($M_c = 4$) and the baseline ($M_b = 4.5$, $p = 1.000$).

![user-ratings-for-steerability-ease-of-use-and-utility-across-both-systems](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2412.10999v4/x4.png)

Furthermore, the way researchers interacted with the system changed. In the chat baseline, users spent most of their time ($54.1\%$) passively inspecting the AI's output. In Cocoa, this passive inspection time dropped to $33.6\%$ ($p < 0.001$). Instead, users were more actively engaged in "co-execution"—interacting with individual steps and refining outputs—which accounted for $15.2\%$ of their session time compared to only $3.1\%$ in the baseline ($p < 0.001$).

![comparison-of-interaction-activity-between-cocoa-and-the-chat-baseline](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2412.10999v4/x3.png)

This shift in activity suggests that Cocoa’s interface encourages researchers to be active collaborators rather than just passive reviewers of AI-generated content.

## Insights from Real-World Deployment

Beyond the lab, a 7-day field deployment with seven researchers provided insights into how such a system fits into actual scientific workflows. During this week, participants used Cocoa for literature discovery and early-stage research planning.

One of the most striking findings was how users utilized the flexible delegation feature. In the field, participants assigned significantly more steps to themselves than they did in the controlled lab environment. These "human steps" typically involved tasks like:
*   Strategic decision-making.
*   Drafting core research ideas.
*   Conducting deep reading of specific papers.
*   Running physical or complex computational experiments.

![examples-of-task-delegation-between-researchers-and-ai-agents-in-the-field-study](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2412.10999v4/x5.png)

The ability to interleave these human steps with agent steps created a "back-and-forth" rhythm. A researcher might have the agent find 20 papers, then manually filter them down to five, then have the agent summarize those five, and finally spend time themselves reasoning about how those summaries influence their next experiment. This workflow acknowledges that scientific inquiry is non-linear; the results of one step often change the researcher's understanding of the problem, requiring a shift in the overall plan.

## Significance of the Work

The research on Cocoa offers a potential path forward for the design of AI systems in expert domains. By treating planning and execution as intertwined processes rather than separate stages, the system allows for a higher degree of transparency and control.

From a practical perspective, the "agent notebook" pattern provides several benefits:
1.  **Safety and Accountability:** By seeing a plan before it is executed, users can identify potential harms or inaccuracies early.
2.  **Resource Efficiency:** Stepwise execution prevents agents from wasting computational resources (and money) on long-running tasks based on a flawed initial plan.
3.  **Preservation of Agency:** Allowing users to "take back" specific parts of a task ensures that humans remain the primary drivers of intellectual work.

In summary, Cocoa demonstrates that the most effective AI agents may not be the most autonomous ones, but rather those that are best at collaborating with humans. By providing a structured yet flexible environment for co-planning and co-execution, Cocoa helps researchers harness the power of LLMs while maintaining the rigor and intuition that define scientific discovery.
