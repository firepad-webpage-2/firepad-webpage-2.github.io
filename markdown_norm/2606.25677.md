## Representations of Monoids and Wreath Products

The study of monoid representations is a foundational area of algebra that seeks to understand the structure of monoids by examining their actions on vector spaces. Specifically, researchers investigate the properties of the complex monoid algebra $\mathbb{C}M$, which is the vector space spanned by the elements of a finite monoid $M$ equipped with a multiplication derived from the monoid operation. One of the most significant invariants of such an algebra is its ordinary quiver—a directed graph where vertices represent simple modules (the basic building blocks of the representation theory) and arrows represent extensions between them.

The monoid of all partial functions on an $n$-element set, denoted $\mathrm{PT}_n$, is a classical object in semigroup theory. Its representation theory is well-documented, but a more complex challenge arises when considering the wreath product of a finite group $G$ with $\mathrm{PT}_n$. This structure, $G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n$, appears naturally in various contexts, including the Krohn-Rhodes decomposition theory of finite automata. This research provides a comprehensive description of the ordinary quiver and the global dimension for the algebra $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n)$, filling a gap in the literature regarding how the group $G$ influences the overall algebraic structure.

## The EI-Category Framework

To analyze the algebra $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n)$, the paper employs a modern technique involving Ehresmann categories. It is shown that the monoid algebra $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n)$ is isomorphic to the algebra of an associated category $\mathcal{C}(M)$. In this case, the associated category is an EI-category (where every Endomorphism is an Isomorphism).

The significance of this approach is that the representation theory of an EI-category algebra is largely determined by the representation theory of its endomorphism groups. For the category associated with $G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n$, these endomorphism groups are the wreath products $G \wr S_k$, where $S_k$ is the symmetric group on $k$ elements, for $k \in \{0, \dots, n\}$. This reduction allows the problem of finding the quiver of a complex monoid to be translated into the more manageable problem of analyzing group representations and their interactions.

To simplify the calculations, the research works with a "skeleton" of the category, denoted $G \wr \mathrm{SE}_n$. A skeleton is a smaller version of the category that contains exactly one object from each isomorphism class. For this category, the objects are the sets $[k] = \{1, \dots, k\}$ for $0 \leq k \leq n$. The vertices of the quiver are then indexed by the simple modules of the groups $G \wr S_k$.

## Structure of the Quiver for $G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n$

The simple modules of $G \wr S_k$ are well-known to be indexed by sequences of Young diagrams (multi-Young diagrams) $(\lambda^{(1)}, \dots, \lambda^{(l)})$, where $l$ is the number of conjugacy classes of $G$ and the total number of boxes in all diagrams is $k$. Consequently, the vertices of the quiver are these multi-Young diagrams across all levels $k$ from $0$ to $n$.

The study determines that arrows in the quiver only exist between consecutive levels—specifically from a vertex $V$ at level $k+1$ to a vertex $U$ at level $k$. The number of arrows is calculated using the theory of irreducible morphisms within the EI-category and the application of Frobenius reciprocity.

The connections between vertices are governed by "branching rules" that involve adding or removing boxes from the Young diagrams. However, unlike the standard case for $\mathrm{PT}_n$ (where $G$ is trivial), the group $G$ introduces multiplicities based on its own representation theory. Specifically, the number of arrows depends on how simple $G$-modules (let's call them $U_i, U_j, U_r$) interact under tensor products:

1.  **Tensor Product Multiplicities**: $m_r^{i,j} = \langle U_r, U_i \otimes U_j \rangle_G$ represents the multiplicity of $U_r$ in the tensor product of $U_i$ and $U_j$.
2.  **Symmetric and Alternating Squares**: $m_{+,r}^i = \langle U_r, \mathrm{Sym}^2 U_i \rangle_G$ and $m_{-,r}^i = \langle U_r, \mathrm{Alt}^2 U_i \rangle_G$ represent the multiplicities in the symmetric and alternating squares of a module $U_i$.

The total number of arrows from $V \in \mathrm{Irr}(G \wr S_{k+1})$ to $U \in \mathrm{Irr}(G \wr S_k)$ is the sum of these multiplicities over all valid ways to transform the multi-Young diagram of $U$ into that of $V$ by removing one box and adding two boxes in a manner consistent with these group-theoretic rules.

## Global Dimension of the Algebra

Beyond the quiver, the paper investigates the homological complexity of $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n)$ by calculating its global dimension. The global dimension of an algebra is a measure of the maximum length of a minimal projective resolution of any module. It provides insight into the "smoothness" of the algebra's representation category.

The main result established is that the global dimension of $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n)$ is exactly $n-1$. This matches the global dimension of the simpler algebra $\mathbb{C}\mathrm{PT}_n$. To prove this, the author introduces an "inflation functor" that maps modules of the category algebra $\mathbb{C}E_n$ to modules of $\mathbb{C}(G \wr E_n)$.

The author demonstrates that this inflation functor preserves several critical properties:
- It maps indecomposable projective modules to indecomposable projective modules.
- It preserves the projective dimension of modules.

By leveraging the known fact that the global dimension of $\mathbb{C}\mathrm{PT}_n$ (or rather, its skeletal category algebra $\mathbb{C}E_n$) is $n-1$, and showing that the longest path in the quiver of $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n)$ is also $n-1$, the author rigorously concludes the result for the wreath product algebra.

## Wreath Products with Order-Preserving Functions

The research extends its methodology to the monoid of order-preserving partial functions, $\mathrm{PO}_n$. While similar to $\mathrm{PT}_n$, the order-preserving condition significantly restricts the possible morphisms. In the wreath product $G \wr \mathrm{PO}_n$, the endomorphism groups of the associated skeletal category are much simpler: they are the direct products $G^k$ (the product of $k$ copies of $G$) instead of the more complex wreath products $G \wr S_k$.

This change in endomorphism groups leads to a different quiver structure. The vertices for the quiver of $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PO}_n)$ are indexed by sequences of simple $G$-modules $(U_1, \dots, U_k)$. The arrows, again level-lowering from $k+1$ to $k$, are defined by a formula involving Kronecker deltas and tensor product multiplicities. 

For a vertex $V = V_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes V_{k+1}$ and $U = U_1 \boxtimes \cdots \boxtimes U_k$, the number of arrows is:
$$\sum_{i=1}^k \left( \prod_{r=1}^{i-1} \delta_{U_r, V_r} \cdot \langle U_i, V_i \otimes V_{i+1} \rangle_G \cdot \prod_{r=i+1}^k \delta_{U_r, V_{r+1}} \right)$$

This formula indicates that an arrow exists if $U$ can be formed by "merging" two adjacent components of $V$ (say $V_i$ and $V_{i+1}$) into a single component $U_i$ via a tensor product, while all other components remain identical.

## Conclusions and Implications

This work provides a detailed characterization of the representation-theoretic properties of two significant families of monoid algebras. By utilizing the framework of EI-categories, the author successfully reduced complex monoid-theoretic problems into calculations involving group representations and tensor products.

The primary contributions include:
- A complete description of the vertices and arrows of the ordinary quiver for $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n)$ and $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PO}_n)$.
- The identification of branching rules that incorporate the representation theory of the group $G$.
- The proof that the global dimension of $\mathbb{C}(G \wr \mathrm{PT}_n)$ is $n-1$, establishing that the addition of the group $G$ through a wreath product does not increase the homological dimension of the partial transformation monoid algebra.

These results deepen the understanding of how groups and monoids interact within a wreath product and provide a template for calculating quivers for other classes of Ehresmann and EI-categories. The explicit formulas for the number of arrows enable future researchers to construct the representation theory of these algebras for any specific finite group $G$.
