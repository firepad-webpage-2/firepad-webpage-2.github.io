In modern wireless communication, base stations must serve multiple users simultaneously by precisely shaping the signals sent from their antenna arrays. This process, known as precoding, is essential for maximizing the sum-rate—the total speed at which data is delivered to all users. Traditionally, precoding involves solving complex optimization problems using iterative algorithms. While accurate, these methods often require significant computational time, making them difficult to use in real-time as channel conditions change rapidly.

Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) offer a promising alternative by learning the mapping from channel conditions to optimal precoders offline. Once trained, they can generate solutions almost instantaneously. However, standard DNNs often struggle with "sample efficiency," requiring massive amounts of data to learn the underlying physics of wireless signals. To address this, researchers have begun embedding mathematical symmetries, or "inductive biases," directly into network architectures. This paper introduces a previously overlooked symmetry—Conjugation Equivariance (CE)—and provides a framework for integrating it into neural networks to improve performance and generalizability.

## Symmetry in Wireless Systems: The Role of Equivariance

In machine learning, equivariance describes a property where a specific transformation of the input results in a predictable transformation of the output. In the context of wireless precoding, several such symmetries exist. For instance, if the order of users in the input list is permuted (Permutation Equivariance), the order of the generated precoding vectors should be permuted accordingly. If the coordinate system of the antennas is rotated (Unitary Equivariance), the precoder should undergo the same rotation.

By enforcing these rules within the network’s architecture, researchers can prevent the model from having to "re-learn" the same physics for every possible orientation or ordering. This significantly reduces the number of parameters the network must learn and makes it more robust when encountering system configurations (such as a different number of antennas) that it did not see during training. This paper focuses on the symmetry of complex conjugation, which relates to the phase of wireless signals.

## The Conjugation Equivariance Property

Wireless channels and precoders are typically represented using complex numbers, which capture both the amplitude and the phase of the signal. In mathematics, the conjugate of a complex number is formed by flipping the sign of its imaginary part, which corresponds to reversing its phase.

The authors formally establish that if a precoder $W$ is optimal for a given channel $H$, then the conjugate of that precoder, $W^*$, is the optimal solution for the conjugated channel $H^*$. Mathematically, an optimal policy $W_{opt}(\cdot)$ satisfies:

$$
W_{opt}(H^*) = (W_{opt}(H))^*
$$

This property, Conjugation Equivariance (CE), implies that the fundamental logic used to calculate a precoder should not change if the entire system's phase is mirrored. Prior to this work, while other symmetries like permutation and rotation were well-studied, CE had not been explicitly integrated into the design of precoder-learning networks.

## Designing the Conjugate Equivariant Neural Network (CENN)

To create a network that inherently respects CE, every layer must be designed such that if the input is conjugated, the output is also conjugated. A standard neural network layer typically transforms an input $d$ into an output $y$ through a processing function $\psi(\cdot)$ and an activation function $\sigma(\cdot)$:

$$
y = \sigma(\psi(d))
$$

For the layer to be conjugate equivariant, it must satisfy the condition $\sigma(\psi(d^*)) = \sigma(\psi(d))^*$.

### Overcoming the Limitations of Linear Layers
The researchers first analyzed what happens if you try to enforce CE on a standard linear layer, where $\psi(d) = Wd + b$. They found that for the layer to be CE, the weights $W$ and biases $b$ must be purely real-valued. However, this creates a significant problem: if $W$ and $b$ are real, the real and imaginary parts of the output are calculated independently:

$$
\text{Re}\{y\} = W\text{Re}\{d\} + b
$$
$$
\text{Im}\{y\} = W\text{Im}\{d\}
$$

In wireless communication, the optimal precoder depends on the complex interaction (coupling) between the real and imaginary parts of the channel. By forcing the weights to be real, the network loses its ability to model these interactions, which severely limits its performance. This finding highlights why simply restricting standard layers is insufficient for precoder learning.

### A General Nonlinear Construction
To solve this, the authors proposed a nonlinear construction method. Instead of restricting the weights, they took an arbitrary base function $\phi(\cdot)$ (which could be a standard complex-valued layer) and transformed it into a CE-compliant function $\psi(\cdot)$ using the following formula:

$$
\psi(d) = \frac{1}{2} [\phi(d) + \phi(d^*)^*]
$$

This construction ensures that $\psi(d^*) = \psi(d)^*$ regardless of the internal parameters of $\phi(\cdot)$. Because $\phi(\cdot)$ can still use complex weights, the network retains the ability to model the essential coupling between the real and imaginary components of the signal. 

To complete the design, the researchers used a specific activation function that is also conjugate equivariant:

$$
\sigma(x) = \frac{x}{1 + |x|}
$$

This combination allows the network to maintain the CE property across all its layers without adding any new learnable parameters.

## Joint Exploitation of Symmetries

One of the most significant aspects of this work is that the proposed CE construction does not interfere with other types of equivariance. Modern high-performance networks often already use Permutation Equivariance (PE), Unitary Equivariance (UE), or Torus Equivariance (TE). 

The researchers proved that if the base function $\phi(\cdot)$ already possesses one of these properties (like PE), the resulting CE-transformed function $\psi(\cdot)$ will possess both. This allows for the creation of "Composite" Equivariant Networks. For example, a network can be designed to be simultaneously:
1.  **Permutation Equivariant:** Treating all users fairly regardless of their index.
2.  **Unitary Equivariant:** Remaining consistent regardless of the antenna coordinate system.
3.  **Conjugate Equivariant:** Maintaining logic across phase inversions.

By stacking these symmetries, the search space for the neural network is greatly narrowed, allowing it to find optimal solutions much faster and with significantly less training data.

## Experimental Results and Performance Analysis

The researchers evaluated their Conjugate Equivariant Neural Network (CENN) framework in two primary scenarios: Fully Digital Precoding and Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS)-aided precoding.

### Fully Digital Precoding
In a system with $K = 16$ users and $N_t = 32$ antennas, the authors compared standard equivariant networks (like UPNN and MGNN) with their CE-enhanced versions (CUPNN and CMGNN). 
*   **Data Efficiency:** The CE-enhanced networks reached 90% of the performance of traditional iterative algorithms (WMMSE) with far fewer training samples. For example, CUPNN achieved high performance even when trained on very small datasets where standard models struggled.
*   **Generalization:** When a network trained on a 32-antenna system was tested on a 64-antenna system, the CE-enhanced version maintained significantly higher sum-rates than the non-CE version.

### RIS-Aided Precoding
RIS-aided systems are more complex because they involve an extra set of parameters: the phase shifts of the reflecting elements. The researchers applied their CE construction to specialized networks like the Unitary-Torus-Permutation Neural Network (UTPNN) to create the CUTPNN.

![performance-comparison-for-ris-aided-precoding-showing-the-advantage-of-joint-ce-pe-ue-and-te-exploitation](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2608.13941v1/img-1.jpeg)

As shown in the results, the joint exploitation of all four symmetries (CE, PE, UE, and TE) allowed the CUTPNN to outperform all other learning-based methods. While the CE construction adds a small amount of computational overhead during the "inference" stage (calculating the precoder), the researchers noted that the reduction in training time and the increase in accuracy far outweigh this minor latency.

## Conclusion and Significance

This research identifies Conjugation Equivariance as a fundamental property of optimal precoding and provides a robust mathematical framework to embed this property into neural networks. By moving beyond simple real-valued weight constraints and proposing a nonlinear construction, the authors created a method that improves the performance of deep learning models without increasing their complexity or the number of parameters.

The significance of this work lies in its "plug-and-play" nature. The proposed CE transformation can be applied to almost any existing neural network architecture used in wireless signal processing. As wireless networks move toward 6G and beyond, where real-time optimization and extreme data efficiency are paramount, the ability to leverage the inherent mathematical symmetries of the physical world through frameworks like CENN will be a vital tool for engineers and researchers.
