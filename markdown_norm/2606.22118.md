## Foundations of Weighted Automated Market Makers

The evolution of Decentralized Finance (DeFi) has been largely driven by Automated Market Makers (AMMs), which are decentralized protocols that use mathematical formulas to price assets and provide liquidity. At the heart of most AMMs lies a "constant function invariant"—a rule that ensures the product, sum, or mean of the assets in a liquidity pool remains constant relative to some value during trades. 

While the most common AMMs, like Uniswap v2, use a simple constant product formula ($x \cdot y = k$), more advanced protocols like Balancer utilize a weighted geometric mean. This allows for pools with more than two assets and varying weights for each asset. The core invariant for such a pool is defined as:

$V = \prod_{i=1}^{N} B_i^{w_i}$

In this expression, $V$ is the invariant, $N$ is the number of tokens in the pool, $B_i$ is the balance of token $i$, and $w_i$ is the fixed weight assigned to that token. To maintain a consistent economic model, the weights must be normalized such that $\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i = 1$.

In these protocols, liquidity providers deposit assets into the pool and receive Pool Tokens (often called Balancer Pool Tokens or BPT) in return. These tokens represent the provider's share of the pool's total value. When a user provides or withdraws liquidity, the total supply of these pool tokens, denoted $P$, must be adjusted. The fundamental rule governing this adjustment is that the ratio of the new pool tokens to the old pool tokens must equal the ratio of the new invariant to the old invariant:

$\frac{P_{new}}{P_{old}} = \frac{V_{new}}{V_{old}}$

Historically, mathematical literature and protocol documentation have treated different types of liquidity additions and subtractions as separate cases, providing distinct formulas for proportional deposits, single-asset withdrawals, and so on. This research introduces a unified framework that consolidates these operations into a single general formula.

## The Unified General Formula

The central contribution of this work is the derivation of a universal equation that describes the change in pool tokens ($\Delta P$) for any arbitrary change in the underlying asset balances ($\Delta B_i$). By substituting the definitions of the invariant before and after an operation into the BPT-invariant relationship, the researchers identify that the invariant itself serves as the general allocation formula.

The resulting unified general formula for the change in pool tokens is:

$$\Delta P = P \cdot \left[\prod_{i=1}^{N} \left(\frac{B_i + \Delta B_i}{B_i}\right)^{w_i} - 1\right]$$

In this formula, $P$ represents the current total supply of pool tokens. The term $\Delta B_i$ is the amount of token $i$ being added to (if positive) or removed from (if negative) the pool. 

This single equation replaces the need for specific case-by-case logic. Whether a user is depositing a single asset, withdrawing all assets proportionally, or performing a complex transaction involving adding some assets and removing others in non-proportional amounts, this formula provides the exact amount of pool tokens to be minted or burned. 

This unification simplifies the mathematical architecture of AMMs. It demonstrates that the logic used to maintain the invariant during trades is structurally identical to the logic used for allocating liquidity shares. This clarity reduces the complexity of implementing these systems in smart contracts and ensures consistent behavior across all types of interactions.

## Expanding the Spectrum of Liquidity Operations

Beyond unifying existing formulas, the paper formalizes operations that were previously undocumented or lacked rigorous mathematical treatment in academic literature. These include partial-proportional operations and fully non-proportional operations.

### Proportional Operations
Standard proportional operations occur when a user adds or removes assets in the exact ratio currently held by the pool. For a deposit, this means $\Delta B_i = q \cdot B_i$ for all $i$, where $q$ is a scaling factor. The general formula correctly reduces to the simplified proportional version used in most protocols.

### Single-Asset Operations
A common use case is depositing or withdrawing only one specific asset $t$. In this scenario, $\Delta B_t = D_t$ (the amount of the single asset) and $\Delta B_i = 0$ for all other assets where $i \neq t$. The unified formula naturally collapses to the standard single-asset formula found in existing Balancer documentation.

### Partial-Proportional Operations
This research introduces "partial-proportional" operations, where a user interacts with a subset of the pool's assets proportionally, while leaving other assets untouched. For example, in a three-asset pool (A, B, C), a user might deposit assets A and B in their current relative ratio but not deposit any of asset C. The general formula handles this seamlessly by setting the relevant $\Delta B_i$ values to zero while maintaining the proportional ratio for the others.

### Fully Non-Proportional Operations
The most complex scenario is a non-proportional operation, where arbitrary amounts of multiple assets are added or removed without following the pool's existing weight distribution. The unified formula accommodates these "irregular" contributions directly, calculating the resulting change in pool tokens in a single step. This provides significant flexibility for participants who may have unbalanced asset holdings they wish to contribute to a pool.

## Swap-Decomposition and Path Independence

A significant theoretical finding in the paper is the formal proof of two swap-decomposition theorems. These theorems address how non-proportional liquidity operations relate to simpler operations and internal trading (swaps).

In a fee-less environment, the paper proves that a non-proportional deposit is mathematically equivalent to a two-step process:
1.  Performing internal swaps within the deposited assets to reach a proportional ratio.
2.  Executing a standard proportional deposit with the rebalanced assets.

Similarly, for withdrawals, any non-proportional withdrawal is equivalent to:
1.  Executing a standard proportional withdrawal (burning a fraction $f$ of pool tokens).
2.  Performing internal swaps among the withdrawn assets to achieve the desired non-proportional output.

These theorems establish a "path independence" property for liquidity operations in weighted AMMs. They prove that the final state of the pool and the amount of pool tokens minted or burned depend only on the net change in balances, not on whether the user performed a direct non-proportional action or a series of swaps followed by a proportional action. While this holds strictly in fee-less environments, it provides a crucial baseline for understanding the fundamental mechanics of these systems.

## Applications in Intelligent Transportation Systems

The paper suggests that the utility of these unified formulas extends far beyond financial markets, particularly into the realm of Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) and Decentralized Physical Infrastructure Networks (DePINs). As physical assets—like freight capacity, electric vehicle charging slots, and warehouse space—become tokenized, they can be managed using the same AMM principles.

The authors propose several ITS-specific applications:

*   **Capacity Co-Operatives:** Infrastructure providers can pool different types of resources (e.g., road capacity and rail capacity) into a weighted AMM. The unified formula allows providers to add or remove specific infrastructure components (like adding a new fleet of trucks or removing a damaged rail segment) through non-proportional or partial-proportional operations without disrupting the entire pool's balance.
*   **Dynamic Resource Rebalancing:** Automated agents (such as fleet AIs) can use the swap-decomposition logic to rebalance their resource holdings in real-time. If a fleet has a surplus of charging time but a deficit of parking space, it can interact with a resource pool to "swap" these tokenized assets, ensuring efficient allocation based on current demand and congestion.
*   **Cross-Modal Optimization:** In multi-modal logistics, weighted AMMs can facilitate the exchange of capacity between different transport modes. The unified formula allows for "Selective Capacity Reclamation," where an operator can withdraw only the resources they need for a specific route while leaving the rest of the pool's liquidity intact.

By mapping ITS resources to tokens and weights, the AMM framework becomes a tool for decentralized, market-driven coordination of physical infrastructure. The unified formula ensures that these complex real-world operations are governed by the same rigorous mathematical foundations as digital asset exchanges.

## Significance and Future Research

The research provides a mathematically complete description of liquidity mechanics for weighted geometric mean AMMs. By demonstrating that a single formula derived from the conservation invariant can handle every possible deposit or withdrawal scenario, the work simplifies both the theoretical understanding and the practical implementation of these systems.

The significance of this work lies in its ability to bring clarity to the interaction between liquidity provision and trading invariants. It highlights that the "minting" logic of a pool is essentially an extension of its "trading" logic. This unified perspective is increasingly important as decentralized protocols move toward managing diverse and complex assets, including those in the physical world through DePINs.

A notable limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the current derivations assume a fee-less environment. In most production AMMs, swaps and liquidity operations incur fees that are redirected to the pool or the protocol. Extending this unified general formula to incorporate various fee structures—such as proportional fees or exit fees—remains an open area for future research. Additionally, investigating how these formulas apply to multi-basket architectures, where pools are nested within other pools, could further enhance the scalability and flexibility of decentralized resource management systems.
