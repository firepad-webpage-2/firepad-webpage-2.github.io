In stable homotopy theory, algebraic K-theory serves as a fundamental invariant of rings and ring spectra, capturing deep arithmetic and geometric information. However, direct computation of K-theory is notoriously difficult. To circumvent this complexity, researchers employ "trace methods," which approximate algebraic K-theory $K(R)$ using more computable invariants like topological Hochschild homology $THH(R)$ and topological cyclic homology $TC(R)$. These invariants are linked by the Dennis trace map $K(R) \to THH(R)$.

The challenge intensifies when the rings or spectra involved possess symmetries defined by the action of a group $G$. In these cases, one must work within the framework of genuine equivariant stable homotopy theory. This paper addresses the computation of equivariant topological Hochschild homology (ETHH), a recently defined invariant that extends trace methods to the equivariant setting. ETHH is essential for approximating equivariant algebraic K-theory $K_G(R)$, which has significant applications in geometric topology, such as the stable parametrized h-cobordism theorem, and in number theory, where it relates to special values of L-functions.

## The Framework of Equivariant Topological Hochschild Homology

For a non-equivariant associative ring spectrum $R$, $THH(R)$ is constructed using a cyclic bar construction, denoted $N_{\text{cyc}}(R)$. This construction naturally possesses a circle ($S^1$) action, allowing for the definition of cyclic homology. In the equivariant setting, if $R$ is an associative ring $G$-spectrum, its ETHH must account for both the group action $G$ and the circle action $S^1$.

The authors define $ETHH(R)$ as the geometric realization of an equivariant cyclic bar construction in the category of $(G \times S^1)$-spectra. A critical observation in this work is the relationship between ETHH and factorization homology. Specifically, for a $G$-spectrum $R$, the restriction of $ETHH(R)$ to the group $G$ is equivalent to the factorization homology of $R$ over the circle $S^1$:

$$
Z_{S^1} R \simeq \text{Res}^{G \times S^1}_G ETHH(R)
$$

This identification allows the authors to leverage the machinery of factorization homology, which describes how a local algebraic structure (like a ring) can be "integrated" over a manifold (in this case, the circle). This perspective is particularly powerful when dealing with Thom spectra, which are built from vector bundles over a base space.

## The Equivariant Bar Spectral Sequence

To perform explicit calculations of the homotopy groups of these spectra, the authors develop a multiplicative $G$-equivariant bar spectral sequence. For a commutative ring $G$-spectrum $E$ and a topological $G$-monoid $X$, this spectral sequence computes the $E$-homology of the classifying space $BX$.

The $E_2$-page of this spectral sequence is expressed in terms of the Tor-groups of the $E$-homology of $X$:

$$
E_2^{s, V} = \text{Tor}^{E_*(X)}_{s}(E_*, E_*)_V \implies E_{V-s}(BX)
$$

where $V \in RO(G)$ represents an element in the Real Orthogonal representation ring of $G$. This tool is essential for computing the homotopy groups of ETHH for cobordism spectra, as it provides a systematic way to handle the algebraic complexity introduced by the group action and the grading by representations.

## Computing ETHH for Equivariant Complex Cobordism

The paper provides detailed computations for equivariant complex cobordism spectra, which are central objects in homotopy theory. Two main versions are considered: the general $G$-equivariant complex cobordism spectrum $MU_G$ and the "Real" complex cobordism spectrum $MU_{\mathbb{R}}$ (where the group is $C_2$, acting by complex conjugation).

Using the relationship with factorization homology, the authors prove that for a finite group $G$, ETHH of $MU_G$ is equivalent to the smash product of the spectrum with the suspension spectrum of the equivariant special unitary group $SU_G$:

$$
MU_G \wedge \Sigma^\infty_+ SU_G \cong ETHH(MU_G)
$$

For compact abelian Lie groups $G$, the authors determine the $H$-homotopy groups for any subgroup $H \subset G$:

$$
\pi_H^*(ETHH(MU_G)) \cong (MU_H)_* \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \Lambda(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots)
$$

where $\lambda_i$ are generators of an exterior algebra in degree $2i+1$. 

In the case of the Real complex cobordism spectrum $MU_{\mathbb{R}}$, the result is expressed as an $RO(C_2)$-graded Green functor. The homotopy groups are found to be:

$$
\pi_\star \text{Res}_{C_2 \times S^1}^{C_2} ETHH(MU_{\mathbb{R}}) \cong \Lambda_{(MU_{\mathbb{R}})_\star}(\lambda_n \mid n \geq 1)
$$

where the generators $\lambda_n$ have degree $n\rho + 1$, and $\rho$ denotes the regular representation of $C_2$. These results provide a concrete description of how the complex geometry of cobordism is transformed by the ETHH construction.

## Eilenberg-MacLane Spectra and Equivariant Bökstedt Periodicity

One of the most celebrated results in non-equivariant THH is Bökstedt's calculation for the Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum $HF_p$. He showed that $\pi_* THH(HF_p)$ is a polynomial algebra over $\mathbb{F}_p$ on a single generator in degree $2$. This phenomenon, known as Bökstedt periodicity, is a cornerstone of the field.

The present work investigates the equivariant version of this result for $G = C_p$, where $p$ is an odd prime. The authors utilize the "Tate square" (as shown in {fig:tate-square-diagram}) to decompose the $C_p$-homotopy groups into more manageable pieces: geometric fixed points, homotopy fixed points, and the Tate construction.

For the geometric fixed points, the authors establish a surprising equivalence:

$$
\pi_*(ETHH(HF_p)_{\Phi C_p}) \cong \Gamma[a] \otimes \mathbb{F}_p[b, c] \otimes \Lambda[d, e] \text{ with } |a|=|b|=|c|=2, |d|=1, |e|=3
$$

where $\Gamma[a]$ denotes a divided power algebra, $\mathbb{F}_p[b, c]$ is a polynomial algebra, and $\Lambda[d, e]$ is an exterior algebra. This structure is significantly more intricate than the non-equivariant polynomial algebra.

By analyzing the maps within the Tate square, the authors identify the full $C_p$-homotopy groups $\pi_{C_p}^* (ETHH(HF_p))$ as a specific subring of this large algebra. The subring is generated by the element $b$ and all monomials that are divisible by either the generator $e$ or any divided power of $a$.

This result reveals that equivariant Bökstedt periodicity is much more complex than its non-equivariant counterpart. The presence of divided power algebras and exterior generators suggests that the equivariant symmetries introduce new degrees of freedom and internal constraints that are not visible in the classical case.

## Implications for Trace Methods and Future Work

The foundational computations provided in this paper serve several purposes. First, they demonstrate that ETHH is a computable invariant, even for sophisticated spectra like $MU_G$ and $HF_p$. Second, the complexity found in the $HF_p$ case underscores the necessity of these detailed calculations; one cannot simply guess the equivariant results from the non-equivariant ones.

The authors note that while the $C_p$-homotopy groups of $ETHH(HF_p)$ are complicated, they might appear more structured when viewed through the lens of $RO(C_p)$-grading. This suggests that the "natural" home for these invariants is in representation-graded homotopy theory, which might recover some of the lost simplicity of the classical Bökstedt periodicity.

Ultimately, these results are intended to be the building blocks for computing equivariant topological cyclic homology ($TC_G$). By providing the $ETHH$ input, the authors pave the way for a full realization of the equivariant trace method, which will eventually allow for the explicit computation of equivariant algebraic K-theory for various group actions and ring structures. This work bridges the gap between the formal definition of these equivariant invariants and their practical application in solving problems in algebra and topology.
