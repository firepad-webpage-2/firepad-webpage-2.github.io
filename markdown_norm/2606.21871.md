## Foundations of Prudence in Risk Assessment

In financial mathematics and risk management, risk measures are functionals used to quantify the uncertainty or potential loss associated with a financial position, represented by a random variable $X$. A fundamental requirement for any risk measure used in practice is stability. Stability ensures that if a complex risk position is approximated by a sequence of simpler positions, the risk value of the actual position can be reliably estimated from the risk values of the approximations.

A recently formalized stability property is "prudence." A risk measure $\rho$ is considered prudent if the risk of a limiting position does not exceed the limit of the risks of the approximating sequence. Formally, if a sequence of random variables $X_n$ converges almost surely (a.s.) to $X$, prudence requires that:

$$\rho(X) \leq \lim_{n \to \infty} \rho(X_n) \text{ whenever } X_n \to X \text{ a.s. and } \lim_{n \to \infty} \rho(X_n) \text{ exists}$$

This property is stronger than the classic Fatou property, which is a standard requirement for lower semicontinuity in risk theory. The Fatou property only considers sequences that are "dominated" by some other integrable random variable:

$$\rho(X) \leq \liminf_{n \to \infty} \rho(X_n) \text{ whenever } X_n \to X \text{ a.s. and } |X_n| \leq Y \text{ for some } Y \in \mathcal{X} \text{ (Fatou Property)}$$

Prudence is highly valued by regulators because it provides a "safe-side" estimation. It ensures that the risk assigned to a limit is bounded by the risks of its approximations, preventing the underestimation of risk during the modeling of complex financial instruments.

## Analyzing the Hierarchy of Stability Properties

The research clarifies the mathematical relationships between prudence and other lower semicontinuity properties. One such property is "weak prudence," which applies to sequences whose risk values remain bounded:

$$\rho(X) \leq \liminf_{n \to \infty} \rho(X_n) \text{ whenever } X_n \to X \text{ a.s. and } \{ \rho(X_n) \}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \text{ is bounded}$$

A significant contribution of this work is establishing the equivalence of prudence and weak prudence for a broad class of functionals. Specifically, the authors show that for law-invariant, convex risk measures that are "order-bounded-below," these two notions are identical. Law-invariance means the risk depends only on the distribution of the random variable, not on the underlying sample space. This result answers an open question in the field regarding whether law-invariant functionals that satisfy weak prudence are necessarily prudent.

The study also identifies conditions under which the standard Fatou property implies prudence. This occurs when the functional satisfies "positive monotonicity" and "solidity." Positive monotonicity implies that if $X \geq 0$, then $\rho(X) \geq \rho(0)$, while solidity relates the risk of a variable to its absolute magnitude. By proving these connections, the authors provide a more unified view of stability, showing that for many common risk measures, the Fatou property is sufficient to guarantee the stronger prudence property.

## Systematic Construction of Prudent Risk Measures

Beyond theoretical characterization, the paper investigates how prudence is preserved when new risk measures are constructed from existing ones. A primary focus is the "cash-additive hull." In risk management, cash-additivity (or translation invariance) is a standard axiom requiring that adding a certain amount of cash $c$ to a position reduces the risk by exactly $c$ (if $X$ represents a loss) or increases it by $c$ (if $X$ represents a gain).

$$\rho(X+c) = \rho(X) + c \text{ for all } c \in \mathbb{R} \text{ (Cash-additivity)}$$

Many functionals are not naturally cash-additive. The cash-additive hull $\rho_\Pi$ is a method to transform a general functional $\rho$ into a cash-additive one:

$$\rho_\Pi(X) = \inf_{m \in \mathbb{R}} \{ m + \rho(X - m\Pi) \}$$

Here, $\Pi$ is a reference asset, typically the constant $\mathbf{1}$. The authors establish that if the base functional $\rho$ is prudent and satisfies certain asymptotic conditions, then its cash-additive hull $\rho_\Pi$ is also prudent. The asymptotic conditions involve the limits of $\rho$ as the position size goes to infinity:

$$l_\rho = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{\rho(t \mathbf{1})}{t} < 1 < \lim_{t \to -\infty} \frac{\rho(t \mathbf{1})}{t} = u_\rho$$

This result is particularly useful for verifying the prudence of well-known risk measures. For example, the Haezendonck–Goovaerts risk measures, which are widely used in insurance and finance, are constructed as cash-additive hulls of Orlicz-based functionals. The paper provides a direct proof of their prudence using this general framework, offering a more streamlined approach than previous methods that relied on complex dual representations.

## Stability Under Aggregation and Risk Sharing

The aggregation of risks across different business units or financial entities is often modeled using the operation of inf-convolution. If two entities have risk measures $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$, the risk measure for the combined position $X$ is defined as the most efficient way to split $X$ into components $Y$ and $Z$:

$$(\rho_1 \square \rho_2)(X) = \inf \{ \rho_1(Y) + \rho_2(Z) : Y + Z = X \}$$

This operation is central to capital allocation and optimal risk sharing. A critical question is whether the "prudence" of individual entities is maintained when their risks are pooled. 

The authors prove that if $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ are convex, law-invariant, cash-additive, and prudent, then their inf-convolution $\rho_1 \square \rho_2$ is also prudent:

$$\rho_1 \square \rho_2(X) \leq \liminf_{n \to \infty} (\rho_1 \square \rho_2)(X_n) \text{ whenever } X_n \to X \text{ a.s.}$$

The proof for this result is technically demanding, requiring the use of Komlos’ Theorem. This theorem allows the extraction of subsequences whose Cesàro means (averages) converge almost surely, which is essential for handling sequences of random variables that may not have uniform bounds. 

The study also provides counter-examples to show that these assumptions are necessary. For instance, they demonstrate that if one of the functionals is not cash-additive, or if prudence is missing from one component, the resulting inf-convolution can fail to be prudent. This delineates the exact boundaries within which risk aggregation remains stable.

## Impact on Mathematical Finance and Regulation

This work advances the theoretical toolkit available for quantitative risk management. By establishing the conditions under which prudence is maintained, it allows for the modular construction of risk systems. Practitioners can combine different prudent risk measures through hulls or convolutions with the mathematical certainty that the resulting system will remain stable under approximation.

From a regulatory perspective, these findings support the use of prudent measures in setting capital requirements. Financial stability depends on the ability to trust that model approximations do not hide tail risks. By proving that prudence is preserved under common financial operations, the research provides a foundation for more robust and reliable risk assessment frameworks in both banking and insurance.

The paper effectively connects abstract functional analysis—such as rearrangement-invariant spaces and Orlicz spaces—to the practical needs of financial regulation. It simplifies the verification of prudence for existing models and provides clear guidelines for the development of new, stable risk measures.
