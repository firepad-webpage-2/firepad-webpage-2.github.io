## Fish Biodiversity and the Identification Challenge

Fisheries represent a cornerstone of Bangladesh's socio-economic structure, contributing significantly to the national Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and supporting the livelihoods of approximately 12% of the population. As the third-largest producer of inland fish globally, the country manages a vast array of aquatic biodiversity, encompassing over 260 native species across freshwater and marine environments. Accurate species identification is not merely a taxonomic exercise but a fundamental requirement for national food security, biodiversity conservation, and the prevention of commercial food fraud.

![fish-phylogeny](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.18302v1/fish_phylogeny.png)
*{fig:fish-phylogeny}: Phylogenetic relationships among the nine native Bangladeshi fish species selected for this study. The tree illustrates the evolutionary distances between species, which directly influences protein sequence similarity and identification accuracy.*

Traditional identification methods primarily rely on morphological characteristics—the physical observation of fin shapes, scales, and body structures. While these methods are well-established in field biology, they suffer from limited scalability and reduced accuracy, particularly when dealing with cryptic species or juvenile stages. A more critical limitation emerges in the context of processed food products; once a fish is canned, dried, or filleted, morphological traits are lost. Molecular methods like DNA barcoding, specifically targeting the cytochrome c oxidase I (COI) gene, have partially addressed these issues. However, DNA can become highly fragmented during industrial processing or environmental degradation. Protein sequences offer a more robust alternative, as they often remain detectable and provide high-resolution data on the functional and evolutionary constraints of a species. 

Prior to this research, a significant gap existed: there was no comprehensive, curated protein sequence dataset specifically for native Bangladeshi fish, nor were there standardized computational benchmarks to evaluate how modern machine learning architectures perform on these specific biological sequences. This study addresses these needs by establishing a new dataset and proposing a computationally efficient hybrid model tailored for resource-constrained environments.

## Dataset Curation and Quality Control

The foundation of this work is a newly curated dataset of 2845 high-quality protein sequences representing nine native fish species of high economic and ecological importance. These include freshwater varieties like Rohi (*Labeo rohita*), Mrigal (*Cirrhinus mrigala*), and Pangas (*Pangasius pangasius*), alongside saltwater species such as Ilish (*Tenualosa ilisha*) and Rupchanda (*Pampus chinensis*).

The researchers implemented a rigorous quality control pipeline to ensure the biological validity of the data. Sequences were extracted from UniProtKB and NCBI, followed by extensive filtering. Duplicate sequences were removed, and length filtering was applied to retain sequences where $100 \leq L \leq 800$. This specific range was chosen to exclude fragmented sequences (often less than 100 amino acids) and to prevent the excessive computational overhead associated with very long sequences (exceeding 800 amino acids). This filter successfully retained $96.10\%$ of sequences and $93.76\%$ of total residues.

![sequence-similarity-heatmap](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.18302v1/sequence_similarity_heatmap.png)
*{fig:sequence-similarity-heatmap}: Heatmap representing cosine similarity of protein sequences across the nine species, derived from ESM-2 embeddings. Bright yellow regions indicate high similarity (often exceeding 85-90%), particularly among closely related cyprinid species.*

One of the primary challenges identified during data analysis was the high degree of protein sequence similarity between species. As shown in {fig:sequence-similarity-heatmap}, many sequences share $80\%$ to $90\%$ similarity. This is particularly evident in species with close phylogenetic relationships, such as the cyprinids Rohi and Mrigal. Such high homology suggests that simple sequence matching is insufficient, necessitating more sophisticated models capable of detecting subtle, species-specific motifs.

## Architectural Paradigms for Protein Classification

To establish a baseline for identification accuracy, the study evaluated seven distinct architectural paradigms. These ranged from classical machine learning to modern large-scale language models:

1.  **N-gram + LightGBM:** A baseline where sequences are decomposed into overlapping character $n$-grams ($n=1–4$). These are transformed using TF-IDF and classified using a gradient boosting framework.
2.  **MotifCNN:** This model uses multi-scale 1D convolutions with six parallel kernels $k \in \{1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 21\}$. Each kernel size is designed to capture biological motifs (functional patterns) of varying lengths.
3.  **Standard Transformer:** A 6-layer attention-based encoder using sinusoidal positional encoding to capture global dependencies across the protein sequence.
4.  **Proposed Hybrid (MotifCNN-Transformer + TA-PE):** This model combines the local feature extraction of a MotifCNN with the global context of a Transformer, further enhanced by Terminal-Aware Positional Encoding (TA-PE).

![motifcnn-transformer-architecture](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.18302v1/motifcnn_transformer_architecture.png)
*{fig:motifcnn-transformer-architecture}: The architecture of the proposed hybrid MotifCNN-Transformer model. It integrates multi-scale convolutional layers for local motif detection with a Transformer encoder for global context, utilizing a biologically inspired terminal-aware positional encoding.*

The hybrid model uses $128$ filters per convolutional kernel, resulting in $768$ channels that are projected down to a $256$-dimension embedding for the Transformer encoder. The Transformer component consists of $4$ layers with $8$ attention heads and a feedforward dimension of $1024$. The use of a $0.3$ dropout rate helps prevent overfitting on the relatively small dataset.

A key innovation in this hybrid architecture is the **Terminal-Aware Positional Encoding (TA-PE)**. In biological proteins, the N-terminus (start) and C-terminus (end) often play critical roles in protein folding, stability, and function, while the core domains might be highly conserved across species. TA-PE applies a scaling factor that transitions from $2.0$ to $1.0$ across the first 50 residues and from $1.0$ to $2.0$ across the last 50 residues. This explicitly forces the model to attend more closely to the terminal regions of the sequence, where discriminative signals are often more prominent.

## Performance Analysis and Computational Efficiency

The evaluation focused on two primary metrics: identification accuracy and computational feasibility for deployment in areas with limited infrastructure.

The state-of-the-art Protein Language Model, **ProtBERT**, achieved the highest overall accuracy of $83.04\%$ and a macro-F1 score of $0.83$. ProtBERT benefits from being pretrained on 217 million protein sequences, allowing it to understand the "grammar" of proteins before even seeing the fish dataset. However, this performance comes at a high cost: it requires dual 16GB GPUs, has a high memory footprint, and takes roughly 21 seconds for inference.

In contrast, the proposed **MotifCNN-Transformer + TA-PE** hybrid achieved $79.80\%$ accuracy and a macro-F1 score of $0.80$. While its raw accuracy is lower than ProtBERT, a McNemar’s test resulted in $p = 0.1120$. In statistical terms, this means the $3.24\%$ difference in accuracy is not significant, suggesting the hybrid model provides performance comparable to the much larger model. 

The practical advantages of the hybrid model are substantial:
*   **Speed:** It is approximately 5 times faster, with an inference time of 4.2 seconds.
*   **Size:** It has 42 times fewer parameters than ProtBERT.
*   **Deployment:** It supports GPU-free inference, meaning it can run on standard CPUs or mobile devices, which is critical for local fisheries management in rural Bangladesh.

## Phylogenetic Insights and Error Patterns

The study provides an in-depth analysis of why certain species are harder to identify than others. By examining the confusion matrices, we can see that errors are not random but are deeply tied to evolutionary biology.

![motifcnn-transformer-confusion-matrix](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.18302v1/motifcnn_transformer_confusion_matrix_enhanced.png)
*{fig:motifcnn-transformer-confusion-matrix}: Confusion matrix for the proposed hybrid model. Note the clustering of errors between Rohi and Mrigal, which are phylogenetically similar.*

![protbert-confusion-matrix](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.18302v1/protbert_confusion_matrix_enhanced.png)
*{fig:protbert-confusion-matrix}: Confusion matrix for the fine-tuned ProtBERT model. While it performs better on cyprinids, it still struggles with the high sequence homology between closely related species.*

The most frequent misclassifications occurred between **Rohi** (*Labeo rohita*) and **Mrigal** (*Cirrhinus mrigala*). These two species belong to the same family (Cyprinidae) and exhibit very high protein sequence homology. ProtBERT showed a clear advantage in this specific area, likely because its massive pretraining allows it to pick up on the extremely subtle differences between these two closely related species. 

Interestingly, the hybrid model actually outperformed ProtBERT on six of the nine species, including **Ilish**, **Pangas**, and **Shapla Pata**. For phylogenetically distinct species—those that branched off earlier in evolutionary history—the models achieved near-perfect recall (often above $90\%$). The inclusion of TA-PE was shown to provide a $4.15\%$ accuracy boost over a standard Transformer, validating the hypothesis that biological inductive biases (like terminal importance) improve sequence classification.

## Significance for Regional and Global Fisheries

The implications of this research extend beyond the development of a new model. By providing the first benchmark and dataset for native Bangladeshi fish, the study enables a new generation of automated tools for the region. The ability to identify fish species from protein sequences alone means that even highly processed products can be authenticated. This is a direct blow to food fraud, where cheaper fish are often mislabeled as premium species like Ilish or Rupchanda.

Furthermore, the emphasis on computational efficiency ensures that these tools are not restricted to well-funded laboratories. The proposed hybrid model demonstrates that by incorporating biological knowledge—such as multi-scale motif detection and terminal awareness—we can achieve near-state-of-the-art results with a fraction of the hardware requirements.

For a nation like Bangladesh, where the health of the fisheries sector is inextricably linked to national prosperity, these computational tools provide a scalable, accurate, and accessible path forward for biodiversity monitoring and food security. Future work may expand this dataset to include more of the 260+ native species and explore multimodal approaches that combine protein sequences with other biochemical markers to further improve discrimination between the most closely related species.
