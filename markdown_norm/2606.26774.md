## Hierarchical Forecasting and the Challenge of Intermittency

In many industrial applications, such as retail and supply chain management, demand data is naturally organized into hierarchies. For instance, a retailer tracks sales at the individual product level (SKU), which are then aggregated by category, store, region, and finally, the entire national level. Accurate forecasting at every level is essential for operational alignment; if the sum of individual SKU forecasts does not match the total store forecast, different business units may make conflicting decisions regarding logistics and inventory.

This requirement for the sum of lower-level forecasts to equal the higher-level aggregates is known as coherency. Formally, if $\mathbf{y}_b$ represents the vector of bottom-level time series, the full hierarchy $\mathbf{y}$ is related via an aggregation matrix $S$ such that $S \mathbf{y}_b = \mathbf{y}$. Beyond simple point forecasts, modern operations require probabilistic forecasts—full distributions that quantify uncertainty—allowing managers to set safety stocks and prepare for extreme demand scenarios.

*{fig:overview-of-the-e2etd-forecasting-framework}: The five-step e2eTD workflow: (I) Subhierarchy selection, (II) Probabilistic forecasting of aggregates, (III) Reconciliation of aggregates, (IV) Probabilistic top-down disaggregation, and (V) Aggregation to obtain the full coherent hierarchy.*

A major hurdle in this domain is "intermittency." At the bottom of the hierarchy, sales for a specific item in a specific store often consist of long stretches of zeros punctuated by small, infrequent spikes. These series have low signal-to-noise ratios and are notoriously difficult to model directly. Conversely, aggregate series (like total category sales) are smoother, follow clearer seasonal patterns, and are generally more predictable. Traditional methods often struggle to balance these two extremes, either becoming computationally overwhelmed by the sheer number of bottom series or losing accuracy by failing to model the bottom-level dynamics correctly. The e2eTD (End-to-End Probabilistic Top-Down) method addresses these challenges by focusing its primary forecasting effort on the smoother aggregate series and then logically disaggregating those predictions down to the intermittent levels.

## The Core Principles: Coherency and Scalability

Existing research typically follows one of two paths: "bottom-up," where one forecasts every SKU and sums them up, or "reconciliation," where independent forecasts are made at every level and then adjusted to meet the $S \mathbf{y}_b = \mathbf{y}$ constraint. Bottom-up methods often accumulate noise from the SKU level, while standard reconciliation methods frequently rely on Gaussian (Normal distribution) assumptions that fail for intermittent count data.

Furthermore, large retailers may deal with hundreds of thousands of series. Computing a full joint predictive distribution for such a system using complex neural networks or high-dimensional matrix inversions is often too slow or requires expensive GPU hardware. The e2eTD framework is designed to provide "probabilistic coherence," meaning that every single sample or realization drawn from the predictive distribution satisfies the hierarchical constraints. This ensures that even if we look at the 95th percentile of demand, the numbers remain consistent across the business. 

The e2eTD approach achieves this by utilizing a "top-down" philosophy. However, instead of using the historical average proportions to split a top-level forecast (which ignores variance and correlation), e2eTD models the splitting process as a joint probabilistic event. This allows the model to stay computationally light while maintaining the statistical integrity of the forecasts.

## The e2eTD Workflow: A Five-Step Methodology

The e2eTD method operates through a structured pipeline that shifts the heavy lifting to the levels of the hierarchy where the signal is strongest.

1. **Subhierarchy Selection:** Rather than forecasting all 30,000 bottom series (in the case of the M5 dataset) or 160,000 series (Favorita), the user selects a small subset of aggregate levels. These chosen series must "cover" the hierarchy—that is, every bottom series must be a descendant of at least one selected aggregate series.
2. **Forecasting Selected Series:** Probabilistic forecasts are generated for the chosen aggregates. The authors typically use Exponential Smoothing (ETS) models, which are fast and effective at capturing trends and seasonality in smooth data. Crucially, this step incorporates exogenous variables like holidays and promotions, which are often the primary drivers of demand shifts.
3. **Reconciliation of Aggregates:** Because the selected aggregates are themselves related (e.g., store totals and category totals), their independent forecasts might not be coherent. A standard Gaussian reconciliation step is applied here. Since this only involves a small subset of the total hierarchy, the computation is extremely fast.
4. **Probabilistic Top-Down Sampling:** This is the distinctive contribution of the paper. For each sample of an aggregate forecast, the model must decide how to distribute that total among its children. For example, if a store is predicted to sell 100 units of fruit, how many are apples and how many are oranges? e2eTD uses a novel sampling algorithm to draw these values such that they sum to the parent's total.
5. **Final Aggregation:** Once samples for the bottom-level series are generated, they are summed back up to provide coherent probabilistic forecasts for every intermediate level in the entire hierarchy.

## Probabilistic Disaggregation and Copulas

The heart of the method lies in how it splits a total $z^{(i)}$ into children $(a^{(i)}, b^{(i)})$ such that $A + B = z^{(i)}$. Traditional top-down methods might simply say $a = 0.6z$ and $b = 0.4z$ based on history. This is problematic because it treats the proportion as a fixed constant, ignoring the fact that sales proportions fluctuate and are often correlated.

In e2eTD, the relationship between children series $A$ and $B$ is modeled using a joint distribution $p_{\pi}(A, B)$. To make this work for discrete counts, the authors utilize a bivariate Plackett copula. A copula is a statistical tool that allows one to model the "dependence structure" (how $A$ and $B$ move together) separately from their individual "marginal distributions" (how $A$ and $B$ behave on their own).

The marginals $f_A(a)$ and $f_B(b)$ are typically modeled as Poisson or Negative Binomial distributions, which are well-suited for count data. The Plackett copula uses a single parameter $\theta > 0$ to capture the strength of the relationship. When $\theta = 1$, the sales of $A$ and $B$ are independent. When $\theta > 1$, they are positively correlated.

![effect-of-the-plackett-copula-parameter-theta-on-the-joint-distribution](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.26774v1/img/plackett_theta=10_nb_joint.png)
*{fig:effect-of-the-plackett-copula-parameter-theta-on-the-joint-distribution}: A joint distribution with a Plackett copula parameter $\theta = 10$, showing strong positive dependence between two series.*

To sample the children given a parent total $z$, the algorithm calculates the conditional probability:

$$
p_{\pi}(A, B | A + B = z) = \frac{p_{\pi}(A=a, B=z-a)}{\sum_{k=0}^{z} p_{\pi}(A=k, B=z-k)}
$$

This approach allows the model to respect the discrete nature of the data (no fractional sales) and the correlation between items. To remain efficient, the authors estimate $\theta$ using Spearman's rank correlation $\rho_S$ from historical data, which is much faster than standard maximum likelihood estimation.

## Performance on Large-Scale Datasets

The authors evaluated e2eTD using two prominent retail datasets: the M5 competition dataset (Walmart) and the Favorita dataset (an Ecuadorian supermarket chain). The M5 hierarchy contains 30,490 bottom series, while Favorita is even larger, with over 160,000. 

The evaluation metric used was the Weighted Scaled Pinball Loss (WSPL), which measures how well the forecast quantiles (e.g., the 50th, 90th, and 99th percentiles) match the actual observed sales. A lower WSPL indicates a more accurate probabilistic forecast.

*   **Accuracy:** e2eTD achieved the lowest mean WSPL across all levels of the hierarchy on both datasets. In the context of the original M5 Uncertainty competition, e2eTD's performance would have placed it in the top 1.5% of all entries (11th out of 892 teams).
*   **Intermittency:** The method was particularly strong at the bottom level of the hierarchy for higher quantiles (like $\alpha = 0.995$). This is critical for retail, where the cost of a stock-out is high, and managers need reliable estimates of the "worst-case" demand.
*   **Adaptability:** Unlike static empirical baselines, e2eTD successfully adapted to trends and level shifts because the aggregate forecasts (Step II) were generated using dynamic time series models (ETS).

## Speed and Efficiency

Computational efficiency is a core strength of e2eTD. In large-scale retail, generating billions of forecasts every week is a significant operational and environmental expense. 

The experiments showed that e2eTD could generate full probabilistic forecasts for the M5 hierarchy in approximately 4 minutes and for the Favorita hierarchy in 17 minutes. Crucially, this was done on a standard laptop CPU. In contrast, many state-of-the-art neural network approaches require hours of training on specialized GPUs.

The efficiency comes from three specific design choices:
1.  **Forecasting at the Top:** Most of the computation is spent fitting models to a few hundred aggregate series rather than tens of thousands of bottom series.
2.  **Batching:** During the top-down sampling, the weights for a specific total $z$ are computed once and reused for all samples that share that same total.
3.  **Parallelization:** Both the initial forecasting and the recursive top-down splits are "embarrassingly parallel," meaning they can be distributed across multiple CPU cores with almost no overhead.

## Conclusion and Future Directions

The e2eTD method provides a pragmatic solution to the problem of hierarchical forecasting in environments dominated by intermittent demand. By shifting the forecasting burden to more predictable aggregate levels and using a statistically sound, copula-based disaggregation method, it achieves high accuracy without the high computational costs of deep learning.

The framework ensures that all forecasts are coherent, which is a prerequisite for organizational alignment. Its ability to run on standard hardware in minutes makes it a viable candidate for large-scale industrial deployment where frequent updates are necessary.

Future research may explore automated ways to select the optimal subhierarchy or more complex ways to model the disaggregation proportions (Step IV) if they exhibit their own strong seasonal patterns. For now, e2eTD stands as a robust bridge between traditional statistical forecasting and the scalability required by modern big-data retail environments.
