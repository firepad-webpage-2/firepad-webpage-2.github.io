## Measuring Scalability in the Ethereum Ecosystem

The rapid evolution of decentralized finance and smart contract platforms has placed a premium on two specific metrics: transaction speed and cost. For the Ethereum network, which has long served as the primary layer for decentralized applications, these metrics have historically been a point of contention. The "scalability trilemma"—the challenge of balancing security, decentralization, and throughput—often led to periods of extreme network congestion and high transaction fees. However, recent protocol upgrades have fundamentally altered this landscape.

Recent research by Meghan Ambrosia and Bruce Mizrach provides an empirical look at these changes, focusing on the Ethereum Mainnet and its associated Layer 2 (L2) networks. By analyzing data from early 2024 through projections into 2026 and beyond, the study quantifies how specific technical adjustments have impacted the network's efficiency. The research compares Ethereum’s progress against high-performance competitors like Solana and Polygon, offering a data-driven perspective on the ongoing race for blockchain scalability.

## The Mechanics of Scaling: Gas Limits and Blobs

To understand how Ethereum is scaling, it is necessary to examine two primary levers: the gas limit and the introduction of "blobs." In the Ethereum Virtual Machine, "gas" is the unit used to measure the computational effort required to execute operations or transactions. The block gas limit determines the maximum amount of computation that can fit into a single block. Increasing this limit effectively widens the "pipe," allowing more transactions to be processed simultaneously.

The second major advancement is the introduction of Binary Large Objects, or "blobs," through the Dencun upgrade (EIP-4844). Blobs provide a dedicated space for L2 networks to store data on the Mainnet without competing for the same gas used by standard Mainnet transactions. This distinction is critical; by segregating L2 data from L1 execution, Ethereum can support significantly higher throughput on its L2s without overwhelming the Mainnet's computational capacity.

The research tracks the progression of these parameters through several key forks, including Pectra, Fusaka, and subsequent Blob Parameter-Only (BPO) forks. These upgrades incrementally increased the target number of blobs per block and the overall gas limit, providing the empirical foundation for the observed declines in transaction costs.

## Historical Performance Analysis (2024–2026)

The empirical data collected between January 2024 and March 2026 reveals a transformative period for Ethereum. During the first quarter of 2024, the Ethereum Mainnet supported an average of 13.67 $TPS$. By the first quarter of 2026, this figure rose to 25.78 $TPS$, nearly doubling the throughput. However, the most dramatic growth occurred on L2 networks. The combined $TPS$ of the L2 ecosystem surged from 78.60 to 226.92 over the same period.

Parallel to this increase in speed was a collapse in transaction costs. The median transaction fee on the Ethereum Mainnet dropped by $99.68\%$, falling from a median of $\$3.786300$ in 2024 Q1 to just $\$0.012283$ by 2026 Q1. The impact on L2 networks was equally significant; "L2 Leaders" like Arbitrum, Base, and Optimism saw their median fees decrease by $99.16\%$, reaching a median of $\$0.001512$.

In contrast, while Solana remained the overall leader in raw throughput—increasing from 804.72 $TPS$ to 1,303.46 $TPS$—its fee structure remained relatively stable. Interestingly, by 2026, certain L2s like Optimism began offering median fees as low as $\$0.000027$, which is notably lower than the median fees on Solana during the same period.

## Empirical Determinants of Fees and Speed

The research utilizes econometric modeling to isolate the specific impact of protocol changes. By regressing transaction speed and costs against network parameters, the authors identified several key relationships:

1.  **Gas Limit Impact:** A 10-million-unit increase in the block gas limit is associated with an additional 3.52 $TPS$ on the Mainnet. More significantly, a 5-million-unit increase in the gas limit correlates with a $59.2\%$ reduction in median transaction fees.
2.  **Blob Impact:** On L2 networks, each additional target blob per block is associated with an increase of 10.75 in total L2 $TPS$. Furthermore, each additional blob correlates with a $4.3\%$ decline in median L2 transaction fees.

These findings suggest that the Ethereum roadmap's focus on data availability (blobs) is currently the most effective driver for L2 scalability, while gas limit increases remain the primary tool for Mainnet fee relief. The data confirms that the network's capacity to process transactions is highly sensitive to these specific technical parameters, validating the strategy of incremental capacity increases.

## Forecasting the Crossover: Ethereum vs. Solana

A central question in blockchain research is if, and when, the Ethereum ecosystem will match the performance characteristics of high-throughput chains like Solana. Using linear time trend regressions and incorporating future proposals like EIP-7938 (the "Gigagas" proposal), the study provides specific forecasts:

*   **Fee Convergence:** Mainnet transaction fees are projected to converge with Solana's predicted fees by August 2027, with both reaching approximately $\$0.000204$. For L2 networks, the average fees across leaders like Arbitrum and Base are expected to drop below Solana's median fees as early as October 2026, reaching $\$0.000328$.
*   **Speed Crossover:** Under the current "BPO increment rate" (adding four blobs every 30 days), the total $TPS$ of the L2 ecosystem is forecasted to surpass Solana's total $TPS$ by March 2029. Both ecosystems are projected to converge at a throughput of approximately $1,820 \text{ TPS}$.
*   **The Gigagas Scenario:** If Ethereum adopts EIP-7938—which proposes an exponential increase in the gas limit—the Mainnet could reach $100 \text{ TPS}$ by January 2028 and potentially scale to $2,896 \text{ TPS}$ by the end of 2030. Without such aggressive upgrades, the Mainnet is not expected to reach $100 \text{ TPS}$ until 2034.

## Broader Implications for the Blockchain Landscape

The findings of this research have significant implications for the future of decentralized finance ($DeFi$) and the competitive positioning of various blockchain architectures. The drastic reduction in fees makes the Ethereum ecosystem accessible to a much wider range of users, enabling micro-transactions and lower-value $DeFi$ activities that were previously cost-prohibitive.

While Solana currently dominates the market share for decentralized exchange ($DEX$) volume—holding $58\%$ of the transfer volume in 2026 Q1 compared to the Ethereum Mainnet’s $22\%$ and Base’s $12\%—the projected convergence of fees and speed suggests a shift in the competitive landscape. If Ethereum’s L2s can successfully offer lower fees and comparable speeds, the "user experience" gap between the two ecosystems will effectively vanish.

The study ultimately reinforces the validity of Ethereum's L2-centric scaling strategy. By offloading transaction volume to specialized layers while using the Mainnet for security and data availability, Ethereum is successfully addressing its scalability challenges. The empirical evidence shows that the network is not just growing, but is on a trajectory to potentially match or exceed the performance of its most formidable competitors within the next few years. For developers and investors, these findings suggest that the technical barriers to Ethereum's dominance are being methodically dismantled through iterative protocol engineering.
