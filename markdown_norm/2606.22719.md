## The Challenge of Real-Time Information in Financial Forecasting

A core difficulty in financial machine learning is ensuring that models are evaluated under realistic conditions. In the realm of macroeconomic forecasting, this challenge is often complicated by "information leakage." Leakage occurs when a model is trained or tested using data that would not have been available to a human decision-maker at the time the prediction was required. For example, key economic indicators like the Consumer Price Index (CPI) are typically released with a significant publication lag—often ten days or more after the reference month has ended. 

If a backtest incorporates these official release values on a month-end decision date, it inadvertently uses future information, leading to an overestimation of the model's predictive power. This "fiction of real-time observability" is particularly problematic when evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems, which ingest vast amounts of data to provide narrative-driven market analysis.

This paper addresses these concerns by establishing a leakage-aware benchmarking framework for ranking U.S. equity style factors. Instead of using standard, non-lagged datasets, the researchers curate a decision-time information set that respects publication lags. This includes the use of real-time "nowcasts"—estimations of current data based on high-frequency indicators—as a substitute for unreleased official statistics. By doing so, the study attempts to provide a credible assessment of whether a 7B parameter open-source LLM can effectively navigate macro-driven equity factor rotation.

## Establishing a Leakage-Controlled Evaluation Framework

The study focuses on ranking seven established U.S. equity style factors: Size (SMB), Value (HML), Profitability (RMW), Investment (CMA), Momentum (UMD), Betting-against-Beta (BAB), and Quality-minus-Junk (QMJ). The goal is to predict the relative performance of these factors in the coming month.

To ensure the benchmark is rigorous, the researchers define the target label as the cross-sectional rank of the market-adjusted residual return for each factor $f$ at time $t$. The residual return is calculated as:

$$
r^{\text{resid}}_{f,t} := r_{f,t} - \beta_{f,t} r_{\text{MKT},t}
$$

In this equation, $\beta_{f,t}$ represents a rolling 60-month OLS beta estimated using only data available prior to the decision month $t$ (specifically, for all months $s < t$). By focusing on the market-adjusted residual, the model aims to capture factor-specific dynamics rather than broad market movement.

The most critical aspect of the methodology is the control of the input information set. All macroeconomic variables from the Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) database, such as CPI and Unemployment, are shifted by one calendar month to account for their release cycles. To bridge the gap created by this lag, the model incorporates the Cleveland Fed's daily CPI nowcast. This nowcast is queried exactly as it appeared on the decision date $t$, providing the model with a real-time (though imperfect) estimate of the current inflation environment. This setup ensures that the model operates under the same constraints as a professional quantitative analyst at a month-end rebalancing point.

## The Macro-RAG Actor-Critic Architecture

The proposed forecasting system utilizes a dual-LLM pipeline based on the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model. The architecture is designed to handle both structured numeric data and unstructured narrative summaries, such as FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee) meeting notes.

The pipeline operates in several distinct stages:
1.  **Macro State Construction:** A 4-dimensional vector representing growth, inflation, term spread, and market volatility (VIX) is built for the current decision month.
2.  **Analog Retrieval:** The system searches a 33-year historical database for the top-4 historical months that most closely resemble the current macro environment. To prevent data leakage during this phase, a strict $\geq 12$-month embargo is applied, ensuring the model cannot "see" the immediate past or future relative to the retrieval point.
3.  **Critic Rule Synthesis:** A "critic" LLM reviews these four historical analogs. It looks at how equity factors performed during those specific historical periods and synthesizes a single tactical rule (e.g., "In periods of rising inflation and stable growth, prioritize Quality over Value").
4.  **Actor Scoring:** An "actor" LLM receives the current macro state, the real-time CPI nowcast, recent event summaries, and a buffer containing the six most recent rules generated by the critic. It then assigns a score between 0 and 1 to each of the seven equity factors.

This "Actor-Critic" framework allows the system to learn from historical patterns while remaining grounded in current real-time data. By using 4-bit quantization, the researchers demonstrate that this sophisticated pipeline can be run on standard commodity hardware, making it accessible for practical financial applications.

## Benchmarking and Comparative Performance

The system was evaluated over a 36-month walk-forward period from April 2023 to March 2026. The primary metric used to evaluate the accuracy of the factor rankings is the Spearman rank Information Coefficient (IC). The study compared the full LLM pipeline against several non-LLM baselines, including Ridge regression and a $k$-Nearest Neighbors (kNN) approach that simply averages the returns of historical analogs.

![figure-2-time-series-of-monthly-spearman-rank-ic-for-the-llm-and-knn-systems](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.22719v1/img-1.jpeg)

*Figure 2: The monthly performance of the LLM pipeline versus the kNN macro analog baseline. While both capture similar directional trends, the LLM shows distinct behavior in specific market regimes.*

The full LLM pipeline achieved a mean Spearman IC of $+0.131$ and a median IC of $+0.154$. While the sample size ($n = 36$) limits the statistical power—evidenced by a p-value of 0.11—the performance remained directionally consistent across different sub-windows within the test period. 

Interestingly, the kNN macro analog baseline achieved a similar median IC of $+0.161$ but a significantly lower mean IC of $+0.070$. This suggests that the baseline is effective at capturing the "average" market trend, but the LLM is better at identifying and committing to stronger rankings during certain periods. This difference in mean versus median suggests that the LLM's contribution is most pronounced in months where it makes bold, high-conviction predictions.

## Dissecting the Sources of Alpha: Ablation and Economic Utility

To understand what truly drives the model's performance, the researchers performed an ablation study. This involved testing the model while removing specific components to see their impact.

The results, visualized in {fig:figure-3-results-of-the-ablation-study-on-ic-performance}, indicate that the most significant performance boost came from the inclusion of the Cleveland Fed CPI nowcast. Without this real-time inflation signal, the model's median IC dropped toward zero. This finding underscores the paper's primary thesis: the quality of the information set is just as important as the complexity of the model.

![figure-3-results-of-the-ablation-study-on-ic-performance](https://paper-assets.alphaxiv.org/figures-normalized/figures/2606.22719v1/img-2.jpeg)

*Figure 3: The marginal contribution of different system components. The addition of the nowcast provided the most significant leap in median predictive accuracy.*

To test the economic utility of these rankings, the researchers simulated a "Long-Top-2 / Short-Bottom-2" strategy. In this scenario, the model's top two factors are bought and the bottom two are sold short every month.

The full LLM pipeline produced the most robust results in this economic sanity check, yielding an annualized return of $+4.2\%$ and a Sharpe ratio of $+0.71$. In contrast, the kNN baseline achieved a Sharpe ratio of only $+0.29$, and the Ridge regression models failed to generate positive returns. The LLM's superior performance in the long-short strategy—despite having a median IC similar to kNN—confirms that the model's primary advantage lies in its ability to correctly identify the "tails" of the distribution (the best and worst-performing factors).

## Significance for Future LLM Research in Finance

The research presented here offers a new standard for evaluating AI in finance. By meticulously controlling for publication lags and using real-time nowcasts, the authors provide a realistic assessment of LLM capabilities that avoids the pitfalls of look-ahead bias.

The study's findings suggest that while simpler models (like kNN) can identify broad macro-analogies, LLMs provide a distinct "marginal benefit" by synthesizing historical rules and real-time narratives to improve conviction in extreme rankings. This is particularly valuable for active portfolio management, where the goal is often to isolate the most and least attractive assets rather than predicting the entire cross-section perfectly.

Furthermore, the commitment to open science—releasing the prompt templates, the archived nowcast data, and the critic's tactical rules—allows the community to audit and build upon this work. This transparency is vital for moving beyond black-box forecasting toward a future where AI agents in finance are both capable and verifiable. For researchers and practitioners, the primary takeaway is clear: before debating the "intelligence" of a model, one must first ensure that the information it consumes is truly observable at the moment of decision.
